Customer Logo + Quote Consent Playbook (References & Case Studies)

## Purpose
This playbook defines Redwood Inference’s legal and compliance guidance for using customer **logos**, **names**, **quotes**, and **metrics** in any external-facing materials (e.g., public case studies, website, sales decks, blog posts, webinars, partner pages, press, and social posts).

This page is **restricted** because it contains internal legal guidance and recommended fallback language.

## Scope (what requires explicit customer permission)
**Always requires written customer permission (via consent form or equivalent email approval from authorized signatory):**
- Customer **company name** in marketing context (“Customer Stories”, case study pages, press, event slides, partner pages)
- Customer **logo** (any use)
- **Attributable quotes** (name + title + company)
- **Screenshots** of customer product, dashboards, internal tools, or Redwood Console views showing customer identifiers
- **Quantitative metrics** (exact numbers or ranges) tied to customer results
- Any statement that implies **endorsement** (“recommends”, “certifies”, “preferred vendor”) unless the customer expressly approves that language

**May be possible without explicit consent (but still requires Legal review if public-facing):**
- High-level, **anonymized** descriptions that do not reasonably identify the customer (industry + generic use case) and contain no confidential details
- Non-attributable quotes (“an ML platform team at a Fortune 500 insurer…”) *only if* there is no identifying information and no confidential facts

**Not in scope:**
- Private, one-to-one reference calls governed by separate reference-call boundaries and scheduling rules (see the Sales Enablement process). However, even for reference calls, do not share customer confidential information without permission.

## Definitions
- **Logo use:** any use of the customer’s wordmark, icon, or brand assets.
- **Quote:** any statement attributed to a specific individual or the company.
- **Metric:** any quantitative statement about performance, cost, reliability, security posture, or business outcomes (e.g., p95 latency, $/1M tokens, uptime, throughput, time-to-integrate).
- **Consent artifact:** a signed consent form, contract addendum, or explicit email approval from an authorized representative that covers the requested use.

## Guiding principles
1. **Use is limited to what is approved.** If the customer approves “website case study only”, do not reuse in social posts, ads, or partner co-marketing.
2. **No surprises.** Show the customer the exact draft (or a faithful excerpt) prior to publication.
3. **Accuracy over marketing.** Every technical or quantitative claim must be supportable and consistent with approved messaging and proof requirements.
4. **Minimum necessary disclosure.** Share ranges and methodologies when possible; avoid disclosing exact architecture sizing, vendor names, or regulated details unless explicitly approved.

## Required approvals at Redwood (internal)
Before publishing any external asset using a customer logo/name/quote/metrics:
- **Marketing owner** confirms asset scope and distribution plan (website, blog, deck, paid ads, events)
- **AE + CSM** confirm relationship health and that the request aligns with account strategy
- **Solutions Engineering** validates technical accuracy (architecture, features, deployment mode, reliability claims)
- **Legal (Finance & Legal)** approves consent language, redlines, and final approved version

Recommended internal approval order: CSM/AE → SE tech check → Marketing edit → Legal → Customer final.

## What to send the customer (standard consent package)
Use the consent form template maintained in Drive (latest version) and attach:
- The **draft case study** (or excerpted sections if customer prefers)
- A **logo usage preview** (mockup screenshot) showing placement and context
- A **metrics worksheet excerpt** (only the metrics requested for publication)

If the customer requests email-only approval (no signature), Legal must confirm the approver is authorized and the email contains all required scope terms (see “Email approval fallback” below).

## Data/metrics disclosure boundaries
### General boundaries (default stance)
Do not publish or share publicly:
- Exact GPU model counts, instance types, or regional deployment maps unless explicitly approved
- Customer traffic volumes, token volumes, spend, or unit economics that reveal sensitive business scale
- Details that could be considered security-sensitive (network diagrams with IP ranges, IAM roles, key identifiers, internal hostnames)
- Claims that imply compliance certification (e.g., “HIPAA compliant”) unless phrased carefully and customer-approved

### Recommended safe patterns
- Prefer **ranges** instead of exact values (e.g., “20–30% reduction” rather than “27.4% reduction”) unless the customer explicitly approves exact numbers.
- Use **p95/p99** latency carefully and specify measurement scope (region, model version, prompt length distribution) at a high level.
- When discussing cost: “reduced $/1M tokens by ~X%” or “improved cost-per-token materially” with clear qualifiers.
- Include a **metrics disclaimer** on public pages.

### Required qualifiers for metrics
Every published metric must include (in the draft or as footnote):
- The time window (e.g., “measured over 30 days in production”)
- What changed (migration to Dedicated, batching, caching, routing policies)
- Whether the metric reflects a specific workload/model and may vary by workload

### Metrics disclaimer (approved baseline language)
Include the following, adjusted as needed for the customer:

**“Results are specific to [Customer]’s workload and configuration. Performance and cost outcomes may vary based on model choice, traffic patterns, sequence lengths, and deployment environment.”**

If the customer requires stronger language:

**“Illustrative results shown. [Customer]’s outcomes are not guaranteed and may not be representative of all users.”**

## Common customer redlines (and how we typically handle them)
The table below lists frequent redlines and recommended responses. Escalate to Legal for final wording.

| Customer ask / redline | Why it comes up | Typical Redwood response / fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Remove or narrow “irrevocable” license language | Customer wants control if relationship changes | Allow revocation with a practical wind-down period (e.g., 30–60 days) and carve-outs for already-distributed materials where feasible. |
| Shorten or extend review window | Customer internal process varies | Default: 10 business days. Offer 15–20 business days for regulated customers. Add “silence is not approval” unless explicitly agreed. |
| Approve logo but not metrics | Risk tolerance; procurement/legal caution | Publish story without metrics, or use qualitative outcomes (“improved latency consistency”) and/or very broad ranges approved by customer. |
| Quote editing restrictions | Customer wants verbatim control | We can edit for clarity but must preserve meaning; customer has final approval of all attributed quotes. |
| No employee name/title attribution | Privacy/internal policy | Offer “Name withheld, [Title], [Company]” or “A [Team] lead at [Company]” if company attribution is allowed. |
| No public company name; anonymous case study only | Regulated/stealth posture | Use anonymized story with no logo and remove uniquely identifying facts (industry + generic scale). Ensure Sales understands limits (no “reverse-identification”). |
| Non-disparagement / competitor references removal | Avoid legal/PR issues | Remove competitor naming and negative comparisons. Use neutral phrasing (“migrated from a previous provider”). |
| Limit channels (website only; no paid ads; no social) | Brand and compliance controls | Accept. Ensure internal distribution plan matches the approved channel list. |
| Require prior approval for every reuse | Customer wants ongoing control | Accept. Build a reuse request workflow (email approval) and keep an “approved uses” log. |
| Add indemnity from Redwood | Legal risk allocation | Generally resist broad marketing indemnities; offer limited IP infringement indemnity if already in MSA, otherwise use standard consent with disclaimers. |
| Add confidentiality obligations into consent | Concern about disclosure | Confirm we will not disclose confidential info and that the customer controls the final draft; keep confidentiality governed by MSA/NDA where possible. |

## Use-specific rules
### Logo usage rules
- Use only official logo assets provided by customer or pulled from their approved brand kit.
- Do not modify logo color/shape beyond resizing; no stretching, no filters.
- Avoid placing customer logo adjacent to competitor logos in a way that implies endorsement or ranking.
- If co-marketing with a partner, confirm whether customer permits logo placement alongside partner branding.

### Quote usage rules
- Quotes must be approved in the exact form used publicly.
- Avoid technical overclaims in quotes (“100% uptime”, “guaranteed compliance”) unless validated and customer-approved.
- If the quote includes numbers, treat them as **metrics** and require the same metric approval.

### Security/compliance claims
Avoid absolute statements. Use careful phrasing such as:
- “Helps support audit logging and export workflows” (not “guarantees compliance”)
- “Deployed in customer-controlled VPC with customer-managed networking” (if true)
- “Integrates with KMS/HSM for key management” (avoid implying certification)

### 2) Approved attribution language (copy/paste)
**Standard attribution:**
- “— [Full Name], [Title], [Company]”

**If customer wants reduced personal attribution:**
- “— [Title], [Company]”
- “— [Team] lead, [Company]”

**If customer requires anonymity:**
- “— Engineering leader at a global payments company” (only if the rest of the story is non-identifying)

### 3) Email approval fallback (only when signature is not feasible)
Use this when customer refuses signature but will approve via email.

**Subject:** Approval for [Customer] logo/quote usage in Redwood materials

**Body (minimum required elements):**
- List the exact approved assets (e.g., “case study web page”, “1-page PDF”, “sales deck slide”, “webinar slide”) and channels
- Include the exact quote(s) and attribution as they will appear
- Specify whether metrics are approved and list them verbatim (including ranges)
- Include the review window and the publication target date
- Ask the customer to confirm they are authorized to approve on behalf of the company

**Approval language to request:**
“Replying ‘Approved’ confirms that you are authorized to approve the uses described above on behalf of [Customer], including Redwood’s use of [Customer]’s name and logo and the approved quote(s)/metrics.”

Legal note: If the customer’s process requires a specific form of authorization, comply.

### 4) Metrics footnote (copy/paste)
“Measured in [environment] over [time window]. Baseline reflects [prior state]. Results may vary by workload, model, and traffic patterns.”

## Operational checklist (Legal review)
Before Legal signs off, confirm:
- [ ] Customer entity name is correct (matches MSA/NDA)
- [ ] Approved channels are explicitly listed (website/blog/deck/webinar/social/paid)
- [ ] Approved assets are explicitly listed (URL/page/PDF title/slide)
- [ ] Quotes are final and attributed as approved
- [ ] Metrics are final, have qualifiers, and match the metrics worksheet source
- [ ] Revocation clause and wind-down period are reasonable
- [ ] No implied endorsement, certification, or prohibited comparative claims
- [ ] Any partner co-marketing is explicitly included or explicitly excluded

## Storage + audit trail (required)
- Store final executed consent artifacts in the shared Legal folder and link them in the deal/customer record.
- Ensure Marketing Ops logs the approval status and permitted use channels in tracking (HubSpot properties or reference program tracker).
- Keep a copy of the **final published asset** (PDF export + page snapshot) in the customer reference folder.

## Escalation and exceptions
Escalate to Legal (Finance & Legal) when:
- The customer is regulated (healthcare, insurance, payments) and requests additional compliance language.
- The customer asks for unusual risk allocation (indemnity, warranties, liability caps) in the consent form.
- The customer requests removal of revocation rights or demands perpetual control over downstream reuse without a feasible process.
- There is any risk that the story could reveal non-public security posture, vendor relationships, incident history, or business-sensitive scale.

## FAQ
**Q: Can we publish a logo if the AE says “they’re fine with it” but we don’t have written permission?**
A: No. We require written permission from an authorized customer representative.

**Q: Can we reuse an approved quote in a new deck?**
A: Only if the consent covers reuse in that channel/asset type. If not explicitly covered, request a reuse approval.

**Q: Are performance benchmarks “public information” if the customer said it on a call?**
A: No. Verbal statements are not publishable without written approval and an accurate draft.

**Q: What if we can’t get metrics approved?**
A: Publish a narrative case study without numbers, or use broad qualitative language. Do not pressure the customer; reference relationships are long-lived.
