Postmortem: SEV-2 Scheduler starvation due to continuous batching bug (2026-03-29)

A scheduler fairness bug introduced in a continuous batching optimization caused starvation for long-context requests when short requests arrived continuously.
A subset of tenants experienced elevated tail latency on chat requests (p99 from ~2.8s to ~9.4s) and intermittent request timeouts (max 1.1%) during the incident window.
08:17 UTC: Tail latency alert fired for chat in us-east (p99).
08:22 UTC: On-call confirmed queue metrics showed long-context requests waiting disproportionately.
08:35 UTC: Disabled continuous batching optimization via feature flag for affected pool.
08:48 UTC: Tail latency decreased; timeout rate returned to baseline.
09:09 UTC: Incident resolved after 20 minutes of stable metrics.
The schedulers queue selection logic prioritized shortest estimated remaining time without an aging/fairness factor. Under sustained short-request arrival, long-context requests accumulated and starved.
Tail latency alert (p99) and scheduler queue depth dashboard.
Feature-flag disabled the continuous batching optimization for chat pools and temporarily routed long-context traffic to a separate pool with conservative batching.
Feature-flag allowed rapid mitigation without a rebuild. Dashboards made starvation visible.
Fairness regression was not covered by perf-canary scenarios; the change was validated only on average latency.
Implement aging/fairness in scheduler selection to prevent starvation (Serving Runtime; due 2026-04-25).
Add perf-canary scenario with mixed short/long arrivals and guardrail on p99 (Serving Runtime; due 2026-04-20).
Update release checklist: any scheduler policy change must report tail latency deltas by bucket (Release Engineering; due 2026-04-15).
