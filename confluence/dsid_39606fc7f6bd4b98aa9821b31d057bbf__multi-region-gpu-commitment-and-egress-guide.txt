Multi-Region GPU Commitment & Egress Guide (US/EU/AP) — Inference Fleet

## Purpose
This page is Redwood Inference’s reference for planning committed GPU capacity across regions, including unit economics, egress exposure, capacity/procurement risk, and SLO implications.

This document was originally distilled from an internal Q3 planning thread about reserving **~8x A100-equivalent** steady inference capacity. It has been updated with **2026 contract terms** and more explicit guidance for multi-region failover and cache rehydration.

## When to use this guide
Use this guide when you need to:
- Choose a multi-region allocation for committed GPU capacity (12/36 month terms).
- Estimate the cost impact of cross-region routing and cache rehydration.
- Decide whether to place baseline capacity in-region vs rely on cross-region failover.

## Definitions (quick)
- **A100-equivalent**: a normalized performance unit used in capacity planning; may be satisfied by A100, H100 (fractional), L40S (multiple), or other approved SKUs depending on the serving tier.
- **Baseline commit**: capacity expected to be utilized most hours (typically 60–80% target).
- **Burst pool**: non-committed or flexible capacity used for short-lived spikes (spot/preemptible or short-term on-demand).
- **Cache rehydration**: refilling KV/cache/embeddings after a region loss or routing shift.

## SLO and product constraints
- Chat serving latency SLO (typical): **< 60ms p95** for in-region requests.
- Cross-region routing during incidents usually increases tail latency significantly and can violate the SLO for EU and AP customers.
- Model constraints:
  - Long-context generation is sensitive to GPU class; cheaper SKUs may be acceptable for embeddings or short-context tiers but not for flagship gen.

## Current region posture (2026)
### Executive summary
- **us-east**: lowest committed unit price, highest availability.
- **eu-west**: slightly higher unit price, but reduces EU tail latency and reduces EU↔US egress during steady state.
- **ap-southeast**: still the highest unit price and most procurement-constrained for large contiguous blocks; keep commitments smaller and use workload segmentation.

### Contract/discount notes (updated)
- **eu-west committed usage discount**: now **9%** on eligible committed spend (previously 7%). Requires territory-specific amendment for new capacity.
- **ap-southeast**: now has a **limited 4%** committed discount for select SKUs only; confirm SKU eligibility with Finance/Legal before purchase.
- Any multi-region commit requires territory-specific contract language; allow **~2 weeks legal lead time** for amendments when expanding in EU.

### Egress risk (qualitative)
- us-east → US users: low
- eu-west → EU users: low
- ap-southeast → AP users: moderate
- **Cross-region serving** (EU↔US, AP↔US/EU): can be high, and often comes with latency and SLO penalties.

### Break-even (rule of thumb)
For a 12-month commit vs on-demand at Redwood’s typical forecast patterns:
- us-east: **~7–9 months** break-even
- eu-west (with 9% add-on): **~6–8 months** break-even
- ap-southeast: **~11–14 months** break-even (varies heavily by SKU and utilization)

## Failover and cache rehydration assumptions
### Rehydration egress sizing
A planning estimate for failover-driven rehydration traffic:
- **EU → US failover**: ~**0.4–1.0 TB/day per 100k active sessions** (depends on prefix distribution, cache hit rate, and warmup strategy).
- This can scale quickly in large incidents and materially impact both cost and performance.

### Latency impact
- EU customers served from us-east during EU outages: p95 can **increase 1.7–2.5×** depending on ISP routes and load.
- If the product SLO penalty (credits, churn risk, or internal error budget policy) is non-trivial, multi-region baseline capacity usually dominates single-region economics.

## Recommended allocation pattern (baseline + burst)
### Default recommendation for steady inference (8x A100-equivalent baseline)
When the workload has **~70% steady utilization** with **~20% seasonal spikes**, and EU latency SLO is strict:

**Baseline committed capacity (target 85–90% of forecast peak):**
- **us-east: 45%** (cost + availability)
- **eu-west: 35%** (EU SLO + reduced cross-region steady-state)
- **ap-southeast: 20%** (keep smaller; segment embeddings / lighter tiers)

**Burst capacity (target 10–15%):**
- Global burst pool via spot/preemptible or short-term on-demand, used for short-lived surges and backfills.

This is an update to the earlier 40/40/20 split: EU remains substantial for SLO protection, but the improved EU discount and updated US availability data shift the baseline slightly toward us-east, while keeping AP commitments capped due to procurement risk.

### Workload segmentation guidance
- Put **generation / long-context** tiers primarily on us-east + eu-west committed GPU.
- Put **embeddings and regional caches** in ap-southeast where cheaper SKUs are acceptable.
- If ap-southeast must run generation:
  - Require an explicit SKU compatibility decision (Serving Runtime owner + Infra owner).
  - Expect higher engineering overhead (multiple code paths, quantization variants, and performance tuning).

## Single-region vs multi-region: decision rubric
Choose multi-region baseline commits when any of the following are true:
1. Latency SLO is strict (e.g., EU p95 < 60ms) and cross-region serving would breach it.
2. Expected failover volume would cause significant **rehydration egress** and customer-visible degradation.
3. The business impact of SLO breaches is material (internal policy: if expected SLO breach cost exceeds ~5% of revenue at risk, treat it as a first-order cost).

Choose single-region commits only when:
- Your customers are concentrated in one geography, or
- Your product can tolerate degraded latency during incidents, and
- Egress costs are proven low (e.g., stateless service or minimal cache dependencies).

## Simple NPV framing (planning-level)
For quick comparison, we use:
- Window: **3 years**
- Discount rate: **6% annual**
- Demand pattern: **70% steady + 20% seasonal spikes**
- Include: committed vs on-demand delta, expected egress during failover, and expected SLO penalties

Typical outcome for chat-like workloads:
- Multi-region baseline commits are **SLO-superior** and usually within **~0–5%** of single-region spend.
- Once cross-region egress and SLO penalties are realistically included, multi-region is often the economically preferred choice.

## Implementation checklist
### 1) Quote and validate pricing
- Obtain regional quotes for the exact GPU SKU(s) and commitment term(s) (12/36 months).
- Confirm discount eligibility per region (especially eu-west and ap-southeast).
- Confirm territory-specific contract amendments if required.

### 2) Confirm SKU availability and lead time
- Request vendor confirmation for:
  - contiguous capacity blocks
  - reserved pool start date
  - acceptable alternates (and performance equivalency mapping)

### 3) Validate routing and failover plan
- Define steady-state routing by market.
- Define failover routing order (EU→US, AP→EU/US, etc.).
- Confirm cache warmup strategy:
  - async KV warmup
  - embedding cache seeding
  - rate-limited rehydration to control egress spikes

### 4) Approve workload segmentation
- Decide which tiers can run on cheaper/mixed SKUs.
- Ensure observability covers per-tier latency and error budget during regional events.
