Benchmark Claims: Legal Review Guidelines (Latency/$ Report)

## Purpose
This page defines **legal substantiation requirements, required disclaimers, and the approval workflow** for public-facing benchmark claims related to Redwood Inference’s **Latency/$ (latency per dollar) Benchmark Report** and associated assets (landing page, interactive tables, PDF, blog post, press/analyst materials, sales enablement).

These guidelines apply to:
- Redwood-controlled channels: redwood.ai site, docs site, blog, webinars, emails, press releases, and social posts.
- Sales/SE assets that may be shared externally: decks, one-pagers, outbound snippets, and “talk tracks.”

## Scope
**In scope:** Any statement that references benchmark results, performance, cost efficiency, latency, throughput, regional comparisons, or “best/fastest/cheapest” positioning.

**Out of scope:** Private, customer-specific PoC results (but see: “Customer PoC claims” below—still avoid comparative superlatives without evidence).

## Definitions (for consistent review)
- **Comparative claim:** Any claim that compares Redwood to a competitor, alternative provider, or “others,” explicitly or implicitly.
- **Superlative claim:** “Best,” “fastest,” “#1,” “most cost-efficient,” “lowest latency,” “highest throughput,” etc.
- **Objective claim:** Quantitative statement that can be measured (p50/p95 latency, $/1M tokens, Latency/$, tokens/sec, error rates).
- **Methodology-bound claim:** A true statement only under specific benchmark conditions (model versions, prompt sets, concurrency, caching policy, region list, time window).

## Guiding principles
1. **Be specific.** Tie claims to the tested set (models, regions, workloads, dates) and link to methodology.
2. **Avoid broad market claims.** No “across all providers” unless we actually tested the full relevant market (we generally did not).
3. **Don’t imply endorsement.** Analyst quotes and partner mentions must be approved and not framed as certification.
4. **Disclose meaningful limitations.** Where results vary materially by workload, region, context length, concurrency, or model configuration, include visible caveats.
5. **Keep raw evidence.** Every published number must have a traceable data lineage to retained artifacts (manifests + logs + derived tables).

## Required disclaimers (must appear where results are shown)
Disclaimers may be adapted for length, but **meaning cannot change**. Use the “Short,” “Standard,” and “Long-form” versions based on surface area.

### A) Short disclaimer (social, small embeds)
**Required text (minimum):**
“Results reflect Redwood-run benchmarks under a defined methodology; performance and cost vary by workload, model, and region. See methodology and configs.”

### B) Standard disclaimer (landing page + interactive table + blog)
**Required text:**
“Benchmark results are produced by Redwood Inference using a defined methodology (workloads, prompt sets, concurrency, warmup, caching/batching policy, and model versions). Results may vary by workload, context length, traffic pattern, region availability, and time. Comparative figures (if shown) are provided for context only and may not reflect competitor peak performance or identical configurations. See the full methodology and run artifacts for details.”

### C) Long-form disclaimer (PDF report + press/analyst kit)
**Required text:**
“This report summarizes Redwood Inference benchmarking conducted by Redwood personnel using Redwood infrastructure and tooling. All results are methodology-bound and should not be interpreted as universal or guaranteed performance outcomes. Measurements depend on tested model versions, serving configurations (including caching/batching behavior), prompt sets designed to approximate production mixes, concurrency, warmup/stability gates, and region-level capacity/latency conditions at the time of testing. Where third-party baselines are discussed, they are included for context with stated assumptions and limitations; Redwood does not represent that such baselines reflect any provider’s best-possible performance. Customers should validate results under their own workloads and constraints.”

### D) Regional coverage disclaimer (when showing region charts)
**Required text:**
“Region availability and capacity can change. Reported regions reflect the tested set during the measurement window; not all models are available in all regions.”

### E) “No SLA / no guarantee” disclaimer (when numbers are used in sales)
**Required text (slide footer or speaker notes):**
“Benchmarks are informational; SLAs and production performance depend on contract terms, deployment mode, and workload characteristics.”

## Substantiation standards (what Legal requires before approval)
Legal approval requires a substantiation package for each asset that includes numbers or rankings.

### Minimum substantiation package (per publishable metric/claim)
Provide the following to Legal (links are acceptable):
1. **Methodology reference**: Link to the published methodology section and internal methodology doc.
2. **Run manifest(s)**: Run IDs, timestamps, regions, hardware class, model IDs/versions, and config hashes.
3. **Raw logs retention pointer**: Where raw logs live (per retention policy) and who can access.
4. **Derived dataset**: The aggregated table used to generate the number shown (CSV/JSON export version).
5. **Validation proof**: Completed validation checklist and sign-offs (Applied ML/Perf lead + Infra/Runtime where applicable).
6. **Repro guidance**: Internal note describing how to re-run the same benchmark (runner command/config) within a reasonable time window.

### Stability and variance requirements
- Any published metric must pass the team-defined **stability gates** (e.g., p95 variance threshold and auto-rerun rules) as defined in the official methodology.
- If variance is material, publish one or more of:
  - Confidence intervals or “range across runs”
  - Notes on observed variance drivers (queueing, capacity, regional network)
  - “Last updated” timestamp and refresh policy

### Substantiation thresholds by claim type
Use this rubric during drafting:

| Claim type | Examples | Allowed without competitor data? | What Legal expects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive (non-comparative) | “We measured p50/p95 latency for X models in Y regions.” | Yes | Methodology + manifests + derived dataset |
| Comparative (narrow, scoped) | “In our benchmark, Redwood was faster than Provider A for Model M in us-east at 256/1024 tokens.” | Sometimes | Identical or clearly disclosed config differences + date/time window + fair caveats |
| Superlative / market-wide | “Fastest,” “best latency/$,” “#1” | Generally no | Requires broad market coverage and strict parity; expect to be rejected |
| Future-looking | “We will continue to improve…” | Yes | Must be framed as intention; no guaranteed outcomes |

## Competitive and comparative language: what’s permitted
### Permitted with proper scoping and disclaimers
- “In Redwood’s benchmark, under the tested workload and configuration, Redwood achieved **X**.”
- “Across the tested set of models and regions, Redwood shows **strong latency/$** performance.”
- “Results are **workload-dependent**; see the interactive table to filter by context length and region.”

### Permitted with extra caution (Legal must review exact phrasing)
- “Redwood outperformed **selected baselines** under the tested methodology.”
- “Compared to publicly available baseline results, Redwood is competitive on latency/$.”
(Requires the baseline source, date, assumptions, and “context only” framing.)

### Not permitted (disallowed claims/phrasing)
Do not use:
- “Best,” “#1,” “industry-leading,” “unmatched,” “most cost-efficient,” “lowest latency” (unless Legal explicitly approves based on substantiation that meets market-wide standards).
- “Guaranteed,” “always,” “never,” “no variance,” “consistent everywhere.”
- “Apples-to-apples” unless we can prove strict parity across providers (hardware, versions, quantization, batching, caching, rate limits, retries).
- Implying competitor deficiencies without evidence: “Provider X is slow/expensive/unreliable.”

## Required content elements by asset type
### Landing page + interactive table
Must include:
- Visible methodology summary + link to full methodology
- Standard disclaimer (Section B)
- Clear metric definitions (Latency/$, p50, p95, throughput, $/1M tokens)
- “Last updated” date for dataset + dataset version identifier
- Export disclaimer: exported CSV/JSON must carry disclaimers or a README reference

### PDF report
Must include:
- Long-form disclaimer (Section C)
- A “How to read these results” section (workload dependency)
- Explicit definition of “tested set” (models/regions/date window)
- Appendix or footnotes with configuration constraints

### Press release / press kit
Must include:
- Long-form disclaimer or a shortened variant approved by Legal
- No unqualified rankings
- Quotes must be reviewed for implied superlatives

### Sales enablement (externally shareable)
Must include:
- “No SLA / no guarantee” disclaimer (Section E)
- A link to the public methodology
- Speaker notes: what not to claim (superlatives, broad comparisons)

## Approval workflow (required)
### Who approves what
- **Legal (required):** Michael Grant or Sofia Mendes for all publishable benchmark claims.
- **Security/Compliance (required for artifacts/data exposure):** Naomi Feldman (or delegate) for any public artifacts, screenshots of dashboards, or datasets.
- **Technical sign-off (required):** Jonas Weber (benchmark lead) or Claire Dubois (methodology lead) for accuracy; plus relevant Runtime/Infra reviewer if the claim depends on system behavior (e.g., caching, kernel selection, routing).
- **Executive sign-off (as needed):** Camila Reyes for press/analyst materials with high visibility; Jordan Blake for sales-critical competitive positioning.

### Required steps
1. **Draft** (PMM/Content): Create asset draft with proposed claims highlighted.
2. **Claim inventory** (PMM): Maintain a single list of every quantitative or comparative statement per asset (include exact wording).
3. **Substantiation package** (Applied ML/Perf): Attach run manifests + derived dataset version + validation checklist.
4. **Legal review** (Legal): Review wording, disclaimers, and substantiation; return edits or approval.
5. **Security review** (Security): Confirm no sensitive identifiers or operational details are exposed.
6. **Final approval + lock** (PMM + Legal): Freeze text/figures; changes after lock require re-review.
7. **Post-publish archive** (PMM/Ops): Store the final approved assets and evidence pointers.

### SLA for reviews (planning guidance)
- Standard landing page/blog/PDF updates: **3 business days** for Legal review after complete substantiation package is provided.
- Press release, analyst briefing deck, or competitor-referencing claims: **5 business days**.
- Last-minute edits (<48 hours pre-launch) are not guaranteed; expect scope reduction (remove comparative language) if timing is tight.

### What triggers re-review
Re-review is required if any of the following changes after approval:
- Numbers, ranks, or highlighted “key takeaways”
- Model list, model versions, quantization, caching/batching policy
- Region coverage or the measurement time window
- Any new competitor mention or baseline framing
- Changes to disclaimers that reduce clarity or limitations

## Recordkeeping and retention
All evidence supporting public claims must be retained per the **Public Benchmark Data Retention Policy**.

At minimum, retain:
- Final published assets (PDF, landing page snapshots, blog HTML)
- The dataset version(s) used for charts and tables (CSV/JSON)
- Run manifests and configuration hashes
- Validation checklist sign-offs
- Source-of-truth calculation code version (commit SHAs) for Latency/$ and normalization

## Examples (approved patterns)
### Example: Safe, scoped claim
“On the tested workload (Prompt Set v3 Production Mix, 256-token input / 1024-token output, concurrency=32), Redwood achieved p95 latency of X in us-east for Model M. See methodology.”

### Example: Safe comparison framing
“Redwood results are shown alongside selected baselines for context. Baselines may reflect different infrastructure and configurations; see notes and sources.”

### Example: What to avoid
“Redwood is the fastest inference platform.” (Unscoped superlative; not permitted.)
