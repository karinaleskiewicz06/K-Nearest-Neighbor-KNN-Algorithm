Dedicated Tiers – Gross Margin Assumptions (v1)

# Overview
This page documents the Finance/FP&A assumptions used to validate *gross margin* for Redwood Dedicated tier pricing (Standard / Plus / Premium). It is the baseline set of inputs for:
- list price floor checks
- discount guardrails / Deal Desk escalation
- sensitivity analysis for burst and headroom policies

This is not a customer-facing pricing document. Figures are directional and used for internal modeling; exact GPU contract rates and partner terms are maintained in the FP&A model.

## Goals
1. Define a consistent method to compute COGS for Dedicated deployments by tier and hardware shape.
2. Set utilization and headroom targets that are compatible with throughput SLO commitments.
3. Allocate support and platform overhead in a way that scales with tier entitlements.
4. Provide sensitivity scenarios that quantify GM risk from burst, seasonality, and supply constraints.

## Non-goals
- This page does not define tier entitlements, SLO wording, or hardware shape availability. Those are owned by Product/Infra/SRE.
- This page does not include customer-specific pricing approvals.

# Definitions
**Committed capacity (Dedicated):** Reserved GPU pool capacity associated to a customer deployment and shape (or HA pair), priced as a monthly recurring commitment.

**Burst capacity:** Short-term throughput above the committed floor, served from tier-eligible spare capacity pools (or temporarily increased batching/queueing within safe latency bounds). Burst is treated as a cost driver because it consumes headroom that would otherwise be monetized.

**Headroom:** Capacity intentionally held unused to protect the throughput SLO under demand variability, noisy-neighbor avoidance (even within Dedicated pools), and incident mitigation.

**Effective utilization (EU):** The fraction of paid-for GPU-hours that are producing billable customer work at modeled throughput. This incorporates headroom and operational inefficiencies.

EU = (billable workload GPU-hours) / (total GPU-hours paid for)

# Canonical Gross Margin Framework
We model Dedicated tier GM as:

GM% = (Revenue – COGS) / Revenue

COGS is computed per deployment shape, then aggregated by tier and region:

COGS = GPU COGS + Non-GPU Infra + Support + Platform/Operations Overhead

Where:
- **GPU COGS** dominates and is modeled per GPU-hour *adjusted by effective utilization*.
- **Non-GPU infra** includes CPU/RAM control-plane components, storage (model cache + logs), and networking/egress.
- **Support** includes Customer Support + SRE/Infra escalation load allocation by tier.
- **Platform/ops overhead** includes oncall burden, tooling, and fleet operations—allocated as a per-deployment monthly amount (tier-weighted).

## Primary pricing constraint
For each tier-shape combination we require:
- **Target GM (base case):** >= 55% (Standard), >= 50% (Plus), >= 45% (Premium)
- **Downside GM (stress case):** >= 40% (Standard), >= 35% (Plus), >= 30% (Premium)

Rationale: Premium intentionally carries more headroom + faster response targets; margin is allowed to be lower but must remain resilient under stress.

# GPU COGS Assumptions
Finance uses a normalized cost index per GPU-hour by class, then applies region multipliers.

## GPU-hour cost indices (confidential placeholders)
The table below expresses *relative* GPU-hour cost (not vendor rates). Index 1.00 represents an A100-80GB equivalent cost basis.

| GPU class | Example shapes | GPU-hour cost index | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| L40S | 4× L40S | 0.55 | Lower cost, higher perf variability by workload; tighter model support constraints. |
| A100 80GB | 4× / 8× A100 | 1.00 | Baseline; broadest supply footprint. |
| H100 80GB | 4× / 8× H100 | 1.65 | Highest cost, best perf; supply risk in select regions. |

## Region multipliers
| Region | Multiplier | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| us-east | 1.00 | Baseline supply + favorable committed rates. |
| us-west | 1.05 | Slightly higher blended rate. |
| eu-central | 1.12 | Higher power + colo premiums; smaller spot of supply. |
| eu-west | 1.10 | Similar to eu-central but with better network peering in some cases. |

## Shape-level GPU-hour cost
Shape GPU COGS per month is computed as:

GPU COGS/mo = (GPU count) × (hours/month) × (GPU-hour cost index) × (region multiplier)

For modeling, hours/month = 730.

# Utilization & Headroom Targets (by Tier)
Utilization is the single largest driver of Dedicated GM. We explicitly separate:
- **Customer steady-state utilization** (what customers actually drive)
- **Reserved headroom** (what we hold back to protect the SLO)
- **Operational loss** (downtime, maintenance, fragmentation, warm pool inefficiency)

## Baseline effective utilization targets
These targets were aligned with Infra/Fleet capacity planning assumptions (see Dedicated tier capacity model).

| Tier | Target effective utilization (EU) | Included headroom assumption | Operational loss assumption | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Standard | 62% | 18% | 20% | Limited burst eligibility; primarily single-region. |
| Plus | 56% | 24% | 20% | Higher burst allowance; more conservative headroom. |
| Premium | 48% | 32% | 20% | Highest SLO posture; warm pool and faster mitigation expectations. |

Interpretation: Premium pays for materially more reserved capacity per unit of delivered work.

## Warm pool / spare node policy (modeled)
- Standard: no dedicated warm spare required in base case.
- Plus: warm spare modeled at 1 node per ~6 nodes (pooled at the account/cluster level where possible).
- Premium: warm spare modeled at 1 node per ~4 nodes; eligible for faster reprovisioning.

These are translated into the headroom component above.

# Non-GPU Infrastructure Assumptions
Modeled as a monthly fixed amount per deployment + a variable component by throughput/log volume.

## Components
1. **Control plane + routing:** per-deployment overhead for orchestration and policy evaluation.
2. **Logging/metrics/tracing:** token/req volume correlated; Dedicated tiers retain longer for Premium.
3. **Storage:** model artifact cache + incident debug retention; higher for Premium due to retention.
4. **Network/egress:** most traffic is inbound; egress primarily logs/exports and customer-region peering.

## Allocation method (v1)
- Standard: baseline infra overhead index = 1.0
- Plus: 1.2 (more observability access + longer retention)
- Premium: 1.5 (highest retention + audit/incident artifacts)

Finance converts these indices to $/month per deployment in the FP&A model.

# Support & Operations Cost Allocation
Support load increases non-linearly with tier because:
- higher expectations on response time and escalation
- increased coordination cost during incidents
- more frequent performance and sizing reviews (especially early in deployment lifecycle)

## Tier-weighted support allocation
Support and SRE/Infra escalation time is allocated using a tier weight, applied to a standard “support envelope” cost pool.

| Tier | Support weight | Included expectations (internal) |
|---|---:|---|
| Standard | 1.0× | Business hours coverage + standard oncall escalation thresholds. |
| Plus | 1.6× | Faster response targets; proactive monitoring for sustained saturation patterns. |
| Premium | 2.4× | Highest urgency; structured incident comms and post-incident review expectation. |

Notes:
- We intentionally keep the model simple; we revisit weights quarterly using actual case volume (Support tickets + incident participations) tagged by tier.
- This page does not define exact SLA response times; see tier definition docs.

# Burst Policy Cost Treatment
Burst is modeled as a *capacity reserve* cost rather than a pure variable compute cost, because we must provision/hold capacity to make burst credible.

## Burst reserve assumptions (base case)
| Tier | Burst eligibility (modeled) | Incremental headroom reserved for burst | Billing assumption |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Standard | Limited (best-effort) | +0% | No separate burst fee assumed in base case. |
| Plus | Yes | +6% EU impact | Burst metering available; pricing TBD in packaging doc. |
| Premium | Yes (priority) | +10% EU impact | Burst is expected; assume some monetization via higher list price. |

How it is applied:
- For Plus/Premium, we reduce EU by the incremental reserve (i.e., less billable work per GPU-hour paid for), which increases GPU COGS per unit of delivered throughput.

# Sensitivity Analysis
We run sensitivities to estimate GM downside risk and set discount guardrails.

## Sensitivity 1: Effective utilization (EU) shock
Assume EU declines due to weaker-than-expected workload adoption, regional fragmentation, or higher headroom needs.

| Scenario | Standard EU | Plus EU | Premium EU | Expected GM impact |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Base | 62% | 56% | 48% | Meets target ranges. |
| Moderate downside | 55% | 48% | 42% | ~6–10 pts GM compression depending on GPU class. |
| Severe downside | 48% | 42% | 36% | Often breaches downside GM thresholds on H100 unless pricing uplift applies. |

Action if Moderate/Severe appears likely:
- tighten shape eligibility for tier (avoid costly/undersupplied GPUs in lower tiers)
- raise list price bands or reduce included burst posture
- require longer commit terms or ramp schedules to protect utilization

## Sensitivity 2: Headroom policy increase
If SRE requires additional headroom (e.g., due to measurement window/throughput floor strictness), we model headroom +5pp and +10pp.

Observed effect (directional):
- +5pp headroom typically reduces GM by ~3–6 pts (tier dependent)
- +10pp headroom reduces GM by ~6–12 pts, most acute in Premium

## Sensitivity 3: GPU cost inflation / supply constraints
We model a region-specific multiplier shock (+10% and +20%) for constrained GPUs.

| Shock | Typical trigger | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| +10% | Short-term procurement premium or temporary region scarcity | restrict quoting to alternative shapes/regions; enforce lead times; reduce discounting. |
| +20% | Extended supply crunch, premium colo, or rushed delivery | Deal Desk only; add pass-through surcharge; require multi-quarter commit. |

## Sensitivity 4: Support load spike
We model +25% and +50% increase in tier-weighted support costs (launch period, instrumentation gaps, or increased incident rate).

Impact:
- Usually <3 pts GM for Standard/Plus
- Can be 3–5 pts GM for Premium if combined with low EU

Mitigation:
- invest in automation/runbooks and self-serve observability (reduces escalations)
- bake launch-period premium into early customer pricing (where possible)

# Discount Guardrails (Finance Guidance)
Discounts are evaluated against the *downside GM* thresholds (stress case).

## Default approval bands (v1)
- Standard: up to 15% discount within band if downside GM >= 40%
- Plus: up to 12% discount within band if downside GM >= 35%
- Premium: up to 10% discount within band if downside GM >= 30%

## Deal Desk required if any apply
- non-standard burst entitlements requested (higher burst, longer burst duration)
- non-standard headroom requirements requested (explicit HA/warm spares beyond tier)
- customer requires H100 in constrained region without minimum term
- discount exceeds tier band, or pricing deviates from tier-shape list mapping
