Demo Pricing & POC Credits Guidance (Hosted, Dedicated, Private)

## Purpose
This page helps AEs/SEs discuss pricing during live demos and early discovery without boxing Redwood (or the customer) into premature numbers. It covers:
- How to explain Hosted API usage-based pricing in a demo
- How to position Dedicated commits and what they buy
- When/how to offer POC credits and the required guardrails
- Discounting and escalation rules (what you can say vs what requires approval)

**Important:** This is enablement guidance, not a customer-facing quote. If a prospect requests written pricing, follow the escalation steps below.

---

## Core principles (demo-safe)
1) **Anchor on unit economics + controls, not a single “per-token” headline.** Redwood’s differentiation is production economics and operational tooling; keep the conversation tied to the customer’s workload (latency, throughput, context length, caching/batching, fallbacks).
2) **Use ranges and examples; avoid bespoke commitments live.** In a demo, speak in terms of “typical,” “starting points,” and “we’ll size this together.”
3) **Separate the three things customers often conflate:**
   - *Hosted usage* (pay for what you use)
   - *Dedicated capacity* (pay for reserved throughput/availability + isolation)
   - *Private* (platform fee + support tier, deployed in their environment)
4) **Make evaluation easy, but keep it controlled.** Offer POC credits when there is a clear success plan and an identified technical owner.
5) **Never discount in the moment.** You can describe how we structure pricing and what levers exist, but do not negotiate live.

## Redwood packaging (what to say in plain English)
### Hosted API (usage-based)
**Customer-friendly phrasing:**
- “Hosted is usage-based. You pay for what you generate—tokens for text, units for embeddings/reranking—plus you get the Console to understand cost/latency drivers.”
- “Because we optimize batching and caching, the effective cost for the same product experience can be materially lower than a naive deployment. We’ll measure it on your traffic shape.”

**Demo tie-in:**
- Use the Console token-level breakdown to show how: prompt length, output length, streaming, retries, and caching affect cost.
- If asked “What’s your price per 1M tokens?” respond with a range statement and immediately offer to size with their logs or a short POC.

### Dedicated (reserved capacity)
**Customer-friendly phrasing:**
- “Dedicated is a reserved capacity commitment: you’re buying predictable throughput and isolation with SLA-backed performance targets.”
- “It’s the right fit when you care about predictable latency under load, avoiding noisy neighbor risk, or when a platform team needs guardrails across many internal apps.”

**Demo tie-in:**
- Position Dedicated controls as: predictable quota, reserved concurrency/throughput, and policy-based routing/fallbacks.
- Use the “parallel demos / quota predictability” story carefully: it’s about production predictability, not a promise that Hosted can’t handle load.

### Private (VPC / on-prem)
**Customer-friendly phrasing:**
- “Private is our platform deployed into your environment (VPC/on-prem). Pricing is typically a platform license plus a support tier, and the underlying compute is customer-managed.”
- “We’ll align on security controls (SSO/SAML, RBAC, audit logs, retention, KMS/HSM integrations) and then scope deployment and support.”

**Demo tie-in:**
- Keep Private discussions narrative + architecture screenshots. Do not promise specific timelines, regions, or compliance certifications beyond what’s in the approved Security FAQ.

## Demo talk track: recommended sequence
### 1) If pricing comes up early (first 10 minutes)
**Goal:** Defer to value + measurement.

Suggested response:
- “Happy to cover pricing. The most accurate way to price LLM inference is based on your traffic shape—context length, output length, concurrency, and quality constraints. What we can do today is show you the levers in Redwood that control cost and latency, and then we can size it with either a quick traffic sample or a short POC.”

Then ask **one** sizing question:
- “Roughly how many requests per second at peak, and what’s your typical input/output token size?”

### 2) During the ‘production economics’ section
**Goal:** Connect dollars to knobs.

Suggested response:
- “Rather than quoting a single per-token number, we focus on giving you predictable economics: batching, caching, and routing policies you can tune—and the Console makes the tradeoffs visible.”

### 3) If they ask Hosted vs Dedicated
**Goal:** Qualify without pitching too hard.

Suggested response:
- “Hosted is great for fast iteration and most production workloads. Dedicated is for teams that want reserved throughput, isolation, and SLA guarantees. We can start Hosted for evaluation and move to Dedicated as the workload stabilizes.”

## What numbers you may (and may not) use
### Allowed in live demos
- **Relative statements:** “We often see X–Y% improvement” ONLY if the metric is in the approved proof points doc and you include the caveat “depends on workload.”
- **Ranges:** “Pricing typically depends on volume and model; we’ll quote a tier once we know your token volume and latency targets.”
- **POC construct:** “We can offer time-bound evaluation credits for a scoped POC with defined success criteria.”

### Not allowed in live demos
- Specific customer-specific discounts (“We can do 30% off”) 
- Written pricing over email without approval 
- Implied guaranteed performance/cost numbers without workload caveats 
- Any “list price” tables or per-model rates unless you are using an explicitly approved, current pricing sheet

**Rule of thumb:** If it feels like procurement could forward it internally as a quote, do not say/write it without escalation.

## POC credits guidance
### When to offer credits
Offer credits when all of the below are true:
1) Prospect has a named technical owner and agreed evaluation window (typically 2–4 weeks).
2) We have defined success criteria (latency, quality/eval, reliability/fallback behavior, or cost target).
3) There is a credible path to a paid plan (Hosted scale, Dedicated commit, or Private evaluation).

### When NOT to offer credits
- No clear use case (“just exploring”) and no timeline
- Prospect is only shopping for free inference / benchmarking vendors with no intent
- Request is really a price concession (procurement-driven) rather than an evaluation need

### Credit structure (standard)
Use the following language and structure. Do not improvise numbers.
- **Scope:** Credits apply to the specific org/project used for the POC.
- **Duration:** Time-bound (e.g., expires at end of POC window).
- **Controls:** Rate limits and quota caps are set to prevent accidental overages.
- **Conversion:** At POC end, convert to a paid Hosted plan or a Dedicated/Private proposal.

Suggested phrasing:
- “We can typically support a scoped POC with time-bound credits, assuming we agree on success criteria and a short weekly check-in to review results.”

### Required internal steps (before issuing credits)
1) Create/confirm the opportunity in CRM with POC scope and dates.
2) Document success criteria (1–3 measurable targets).
3) Request credit approval via the standard internal channel (Sales leadership + Finance/Legal as applicable).
4) Confirm tenant/project isolation (do not use the sales demo tenant for POCs).

## Dedicated commits: how to explain without overcommitting
### What a Dedicated commit buys
- Reserved capacity and predictable throughput
- Isolation (reduces noisy neighbor risk)
- SLA-backed uptime/latency targets (per contract)
- Optional burst policies and controlled fallbacks

### Demo-safe language
- “Dedicated pricing is typically a monthly/annual commit based on the capacity profile you need. We’ll size it based on peak concurrency, latency SLOs, and the model/context length.”

### Qualification questions (pick 1–2)
- “Do you have a defined latency SLO at p95/p99?”
- “What does peak concurrency look like—are there predictable spikes?”
- “Is isolation a security requirement or more of a predictability concern?”

## Discounting & escalation guardrails
### You may do without escalation
- Explain packaging (Hosted vs Dedicated vs Private)
- Explain that volume and commitment affect pricing
- Offer to run sizing and provide a formal quote

### Requires escalation (do not proceed live)
- Any discount request (including “match vendor X”)
- Any request for a written price proposal
- Any multi-year/enterprise bundling discussion
- Any custom SLA language or penalty discussions

### Escalation path (internal)
1) Loop in your sales leader sponsor (NA: Stephanie Nguyen, EMEA: Lukas Meyer).
2) If Dedicated/Private: loop in Sales Engineering owner (Markus Klein) for sizing inputs.

   - Deployment mode (Hosted/Dedicated/Private)
   - Models + regions (if known)
   - Estimated token volume / concurrency
   - Required SLOs and security constraints
   - Desired start date and decision process

## Common questions (demo-friendly answers)
### “Are you cheaper than [Competitor]?”
- “We focus on total cost at your target quality and latency. The biggest drivers are traffic shape and the operational levers—batching, caching, and routing policies. We can benchmark your workload quickly and show the full cost/latency breakdown.”

### “Do you have minimums?”
- “Hosted is designed to start small and scale. Dedicated and Private typically come with commitments because they reserve capacity and include support/SLA components.”

### “Can we cap spend?”
- “Yes. We support quotas, rate limits, and usage monitoring so you can prevent surprise bills and enforce team-by-team guardrails.”

### “How do credits work—can we use them in production?”
- “Credits are for evaluation in a scoped POC environment. If you need production coverage, we’ll convert you to a paid plan or a Dedicated/Private proposal with the right SLAs.”

### “Do you charge for retries / failed requests?”
- “Charges are tied to actual model usage; the Console helps you identify errors, retries, and where costs are coming from. For contract specifics, we’ll confirm in the formal quote.”

---

## Do / Don’t checklist
**Do**
- Tie pricing to the customer’s workload and success criteria
- Use the Console to show cost drivers and mitigation levers
- Offer a quick sizing step (traffic sample or POC)
- Keep all demo metrics and logs synthetic

**Don’t**
- Quote a custom price on a live call
- Email per-token numbers as if they are a proposal
- Promise Dedicated capacity availability on a specific date without checking
- Use the demo tenant as a stand-in for customer POCs
