RFP Response Ownership and Approvals (Operating Procedure)

## Purpose
This page defines Redwood Inference’s operating procedure for responding to enterprise RFPs and security questionnaires for Hosted, Dedicated, and Private deployments. It clarifies:
- Who owns each section of an RFP response and who has approval authority
- Which reviews are required (Security/GRC, Product, Legal, Finance/Deal Desk)
- How to use standard, pre-approved language
- How to track and approve any deviations from standard language

This process exists to reduce cycle time while ensuring we do not over-commit (especially on security/compliance capabilities, contractual terms, and roadmap).

## Scope
**In scope:**
- Customer RFPs, RFIs, security questionnaires, supplier onboarding portals, and procurement “security addendum” questions
- Responses referencing our security/compliance posture (SOC 2, ISO alignment statements, audit logging, encryption/KMS, data retention/deletion, residency, subprocessors)
- Deployment-specific responses (Hosted vs Dedicated vs Private)

**Out of scope:**
- Negotiation of final MSA/DPA language (handled by Legal/Deal Desk process)
- New feature promises or roadmap commitments (must not be included in RFP language)

## Definitions
- **Standard language:** Approved, copy/paste answer blocks maintained in the Enterprise Security Enablement Kit / RFP library.
- **Deviation:** Any answer that materially changes meaning, expands scope, narrows customer responsibility, adds a commitment, or introduces new timelines beyond standard language.
- **Evidence:** Audit reports and restricted artifacts (e.g., SOC 2 report) governed by NDA and the evidence request process.

## Roles and responsibilities
### Primary roles
- **Account Executive (AE) / Sales owner:** Owns the overall RFP timeline, customer coordination, and final package submission.
- **Solutions Engineering (SE):** Owns technical product responses and deployment architecture details; ensures answers align to current product behavior.
- **Security Engineering:** Owns technical security control accuracy (encryption, vuln management, IR processes at a technical level).
- **GRC (Security & Compliance):** Owns compliance/audit representations, evidence statements, and “what we can provide under NDA.”
- **Product Security PM:** Ensures capabilities and any roadmap disclaimers are accurate and consistent across modes (Hosted/Dedicated/Private).
- **Legal (Finance & Legal):** Owns contractual/legal representations, liability-related commitments, privacy/DPA references, breach notification language when included in RFP responses, and restrictions on disclosure.
- **Finance / Deal Desk:** Owns commercial commitments that may appear in RFPs (credits, service levels if tied to pricing, packaging inclusions, non-standard support/evidence SLAs).

### Default owners by RFP section (unless the customer format dictates otherwise)
- **Security program / governance / policies:** GRC + Security Engineering
- **Compliance (SOC 2, ISO alignment):** GRC (Legal review if wording could be construed as certification)
- **Data protection & privacy:** Legal (with Security/GRC input for technical accuracy)
- **Encryption & key management:** Security Engineering + SE (GRC for evidence wording)
- **Access control (SSO/SAML/SCIM, RBAC):** SE + Product Security PM (Security Eng for accuracy)
- **Audit logs / monitoring:** SE + Security Engineering
- **Incident response / BCP:** Security Engineering + GRC (Legal if customer requests contractual notification commitments)
- **Subprocessors / vendors:** GRC (Legal review if a contractual representation is required)
- **Deployment architecture (Hosted/Dedicated/Private) & shared responsibility:** SE (Product Security PM aligns; Security Eng reviews)

## Approval authority (who can approve what)
Use the table below to determine when a response can be sent as-is versus when it must be reviewed/approved.

### Approval matrix
**1) Security/compliance claims**
- **Can use without additional review:** Standard language copied verbatim from the current approved RFP library or Security FAQ.
- **Requires approval:** Any deviation or any net-new statement.
- **Approvers:**
  - Technical control statements: **Marcus Lin** (Security Eng) or **Kevin Osei** (Security controls SME)
  - Compliance/audit posture statements (SOC 2, ISO alignment wording, evidence availability): **Olga Petrov** or **Daniel Carter**
  - Capability/roadmap boundary checks: **Naomi Feldman**

**2) Legal/contractual representations**
- **Always requires Legal approval** if the response includes or implies:
  - Breach notification timelines
  - Liability/indemnity references
  - Data processing roles (controller/processor) or DPA language
  - Governing law, jurisdiction, or audit rights
  - Commitments to sign customer templates without redlines
- **Approver:** **Michael Grant** (Legal) (backup: Deal Desk escalation via Finance & Legal on-call)

**3) Commercial commitments / packaging**
- **Requires Finance/Deal Desk approval** if the response includes:
  - Non-standard SLAs, service credits, support hours, turnaround times for questionnaires/evidence
  - Free/discounted compliance add-ons, non-standard retention periods as part of pricing, or “included at no cost” commitments
- **Approver:** **Sofia Mendes**

**4) Product capability commitments**
- **Requires Product approval** if the response includes:
  - Feature commitments, timelines, beta access promises, or “available soon” statements
  - Claims that depend on configuration not generally available
- **Approver:** **Naomi Feldman**

## Required reviews (minimum bar)
### When using only standard language
- AE/SE confirm:
  - Correct deployment mode (Hosted/Dedicated/Private)
  - Correct artifact version references (FAQ version/date)
  - No edits beyond formatting

### When any deviation is present
- **Security/GRC review is required** for security/compliance deviations.
- **Legal review is required** for privacy/contractual deviations.
- **Product Security PM review is required** for capability boundary changes.
- **Finance/Deal Desk review is required** for any commercial commitments.

## Standard workflow (end-to-end)
### 1) Intake and classification (Sales + SE)
1. Create an internal tracking item (deal workspace) with:
   - Customer name, due date/time zone, submission format
   - Deployment mode(s) in scope
   - Requested evidence list (SOC 2 report, pen test letter, etc.)
   - Any “red flag” questions (ISO certification, residency guarantees, HIPAA/BAA, custom breach terms)
2. Decide if this is:
   - **Questionnaire only** (security/compliance)
   - **Full RFP** (security + product + commercial)

### 2) Drafting (SE + domain owners)
- Use the **RFP response library** and **Security FAQ** as the source for baseline answers.
- If the customer question does not map cleanly to existing language, draft a proposed answer and mark it as **DEVIATION**.

### 3) Review (Security/GRC/Product/Legal/Finance as needed)
- Route deviations to the correct approvers using the approval matrix.
- Include:
  - The exact customer question
  - The proposed answer
  - The source link(s) to any supporting internal doc
  - Whether evidence exists and whether NDA is required

### 4) Finalization and submission (AE)
- AE ensures:
  - All deviations have explicit approvals
  - Evidence requests follow the evidence playbook (NDA gating)
  - The final response package includes the correct version citations
- Submit and store a copy of the final submission in the deal workspace.

### 5) Post-mortem and library updates (SE + GRC)
- Any new recurring question should result in either:
  - A new standard snippet (after approvals), or
  - An entry in the exceptions policy if we cannot meet the control

## Tracking deviations from standard language
### What counts as a deviation (examples)
Treat the following as deviations (non-exhaustive):
- Changing “we support” to “we guarantee” (or adding timelines like “within 24 hours”)
- Adding hard retention/residency guarantees that exceed current product controls
- Stating we are “ISO 27001 certified” (unless explicitly confirmed by GRC; use alignment language only when approved)
- Pen test frequency details beyond approved wording
- Any statement about using customer data for training that differs from approved language

### How to record deviations
For each deviation, record the following in the deal workspace (or the RFP deviation log if used by your team):
- Customer + opportunity ID
- Question reference (section/page)
- Proposed modified language (exact text)
- Risk level (Low/Med/High)
- Required approver(s)
- Approval outcome (Approved / Approved with edits / Rejected)
- Final shipped language
- Date/time of approval

### Who can approve a deviation
Only the approvers listed in the approval matrix may approve deviations. Sales/SE may not self-approve changes to security/compliance claims, legal representations, or commercial commitments.

## Versioning and citation rules
- In RFP responses, reference the **current Security FAQ version/date** when applicable. Do not cite draft artifacts.
- Do not attach restricted evidence (e.g., SOC 2 report) outside the evidence request process.
- If the customer asks for “your latest SOC 2,” respond with the approved evidence request instructions and do not paraphrase audit opinions.

## SLAs and escalation
### Default SLAs (business days)
- Standard-language usage: same day (AE/SE)
- Security/GRC deviation review: 2 business days
- Legal review for RFP language: 2–3 business days (faster for small deltas)
- Evidence requests (SOC 2 report): per evidence playbook, NDA required

### Urgent deadlines
If an RFP is due in <48 hours:
1. AE escalates using the Security Review Escalation & Handoff process.
2. If approvals cannot be completed, do not invent language. Use the approved exception/alternate wording and offer a follow-up under NDA where applicable.

## Prohibited statements (hard guardrails)
Do not include the following in RFP answers unless Legal/Security/GRC explicitly provides approved wording:
- Roadmap commitments (dates, “guaranteed by Qx,” “in progress” promises)
- Certification misrepresentation (e.g., “ISO certified” without explicit approval)
- Unapproved breach notification timelines or contractual commitments
- Absolute residency statements that exceed our documented controls
- Any statement that conflicts with the “What we do and don’t say” guidance
