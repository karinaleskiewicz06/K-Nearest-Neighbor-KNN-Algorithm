Private Deployments: Compliance Addendum + Packaging & Pricing Notes (Regulated Customers)

## Purpose
These notes capture Redwood Inference’s current (internal) commercial constructs for **Redwood Dedicated** and **Redwood Private (VPC / on-prem)** when selling into regulated environments (financial services, healthcare/PHI-adjacent, insurance, etc.).

This page is intended for Sales, Deal Desk, Product, and Security partners supporting regulated opportunities. It is **not customer-facing**.

**Key goals:**
- Keep packaging consistent across deals while preserving flexibility for compliance-driven requirements.
- Provide clear guidance on when to involve **Legal** and/or **Security**.
- Prevent over-commitment on compliance claims and non-standard contract language.

---

## Definitions (internal)
- **Dedicated**: Redwood-managed dedicated GPU pools (single-tenant compute) with SLA-backed availability/latency targets; customer uses Redwood control plane and standard API surface.
- **Private (VPC)**: Private deployment into a customer-controlled VPC with customer-managed networking boundaries; control plane components may be isolated based on the offering.
- **Private (on-prem / air-gapped)**: Deployment into customer-controlled data center; higher implementation effort and strict change-control.
- **Compliance addendum**: Contract attachment covering security & compliance obligations beyond the standard MSA/DPA (e.g., audit logging expectations, retention configuration responsibilities, support response requirements, evidence sharing process).

## Recommended default packaging by regulated need
Use this table to anchor the first proposal. Deviations should be documented in the deal record and escalated per the **Approvals** section.

| Buyer requirement (common) | Best-fit offer | Notes / positioning |
|---|---|---|
| Data isolation + predictable throughput; minimal deployment complexity | Dedicated | Emphasize predictable throughput, operational tooling, and SLA. Data isolation is compute-level; data handling is per DPA + product controls. |
| Network boundary (VPC), strict access controls, auditability | Private (VPC) | Use when they require VPC egress controls, private endpoints, strict integration with customer IAM/KMS, and/or internal security review demands. |
| Air-gapped / no internet, on-prem data residency controls | Private (on-prem) | High-friction; require SE scoping + Legal/Security early. Confirm support model and upgrade cadence up front. |
| “Prove it” requirement before procurement | Dedicated or Private pilot | Use a time-boxed pilot with explicit success criteria and guardrails (credits, limited scope, defined evidence pack items). |

## Commercial components (what we sell)
### 1) Base commercial constructs
**Dedicated** (typical structure):
- **Reserved capacity commitment** (monthly/annual) tied to a defined GPU pool size and region.
- **SLA**: availability + support response commitments (see Support tiers).
- **Burst / overage**: optional, pre-approved ceilings (strongly preferred vs unbounded “best effort”).

**Private (VPC / on-prem)** (typical structure):
- **Platform fee** (annual) for Private deployment rights + control plane components.
- **Capacity commitment** (where applicable) for managed compute, or **customer-provided compute** for on-prem models.
- **Implementation / onboarding**: one-time fee may apply for complex networking, custom deployment topology, or on-prem installs.
- **Support tier requirement**: Private generally requires Enhanced or Enterprise support (see below).

### 2) Support tiers (packaging guidance)
Support is a primary lever for regulated deals; default to the lowest tier that satisfies buyer requirements, but do not under-sell support for Private.

| Tier (internal) | Typical fit | Standard commitments (guidance) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Dedicated (mid-market), lower-risk workloads | Business-hours support, best-effort guidance on questionnaires. Not recommended for on-prem. |
| Enhanced | Dedicated (regulated) and Private (VPC) | Faster response SLAs, escalation path, structured security questionnaire support, and coordination for evidence pack requests. |
| Enterprise | Private (VPC/on-prem), strict compliance programs | 24x7 coverage (where offered), named escalation path, incident comms coordination, and planned change windows for upgrades (esp. on-prem). |

**Notes:**
- Regulated buyers frequently interpret “support” as part of their compliance posture (incident response, breach notification coordination, audit evidence turnaround). Ensure the support tier aligns with contract language.
- If the prospect requests named resources (TAM/SA), treat as **Enterprise** (or add a paid add-on).

## Compliance add-ons (what we can package)
Use add-ons to align pricing to compliance-driven scope. Do not promise “included” work that creates ongoing obligations without commercial coverage.

### A) Compliance & evidence handling add-on
Use when buyers require repeatable, fast-turnaround responses and controlled evidence sharing.

Typically covers:
- Defined turnaround target for **security questionnaires** (e.g., initial response within X business days for standard sets).
- Access to the **evidence pack** via controlled distribution (NDA required; Security owns release).
- Support coordination for follow-up questions and audit artifacts.

**Important:** Evidence pack items are what exists today; do not commit to producing net-new audit artifacts without Security approval.

### B) Audit log export & retention controls add-on
Use when the buyer requires:
- Formal commitment to **audit log export** availability/format, or
- Enhanced retention configuration support beyond defaults.

Packaging notes:
- The product supports audit logs and export patterns; however, long-term retention is often shared responsibility (customer storage + Redwood services). Keep responsibilities explicit.
- If a customer requests a specific schema or SIEM integration as a deliverable, treat as paid professional services (or a scoped SOW).

### C) Key management integration add-on (KMS/HSM)
Use when a buyer requires:
- Customer-managed keys (CMK) integration beyond standard encryption controls,
- HSM-backed workflows, or
- Specific key rotation attestations.

Packaging notes:
- Confirm whether request is policy-level (documentation/attestation) vs engineering work.
- Any statement implying “customer exclusively controls keys such that Redwood cannot decrypt” must be reviewed by Security and Legal for accuracy.

### D) Data residency & regional isolation add-on
Use when the buyer requires:
- Region pinning, geo-fenced failover constraints, or
- Contractual commitments around where data is processed.

Packaging notes:
- Ensure the SLA/fallback narrative aligns with residency constraints (e.g., “no cross-region failover” may reduce availability options).
- Any hard residency commitment must be mirrored in the order form and the operational runbook.

## Retention controls: how to sell it without over-committing
Retention is frequently where regulated deals go sideways. Use the language below as internal guidance.

### What we can generally support (directionally)
- Configurable retention windows for certain logs/telemetry (product-dependent) and export to customer-controlled storage.
- Customer-controlled retention in their systems once exported.
- Clear policy documentation describing default retention and options.

### What requires escalation
Escalate to Security + Product if the customer requests:
- “Zero retention” guarantees (especially across all systems) or immediate deletion requirements.
- Custom retention by tenant sub-group, by field, or by request type.
- Contractual commitments that conflict with operational needs (incident investigations, abuse prevention, billing reconciliation).

### Shared responsibility (must be explicit)
For Private and regulated Dedicated, retention typically includes:
- **Redwood responsibilities:** product controls, configuration guidance, and documented defaults.
- **Customer responsibilities:** configuring export destinations, retaining exported data per policy, and setting internal access controls.

## Discounts, credits, and pilot guardrails (regulated ABM defaults)
Regulated pilots can consume disproportionate SE/Security/Legal bandwidth. Use guardrails:

- Prefer **time-boxed credits** tied to a defined success plan (workload + KPI + decision date).
- Require a **mutual action plan** before granting non-trivial credits.
- Avoid “free compliance” (evidence, custom contract terms, bespoke retention) without a clear path to paid production.

**Escalate for approval** when:
- Credits exceed the standard pilot range for the segment,
- The customer requests a discounted platform fee for Private,
- The buyer ties credits to security questionnaire turnaround SLAs.

## Contracting notes (what typically shows up in redlines)
Common regulated redlines and how to route them:

1) **DPA + data processing scope**
- Usually handled by Legal; Security may review technical accuracy.
- Confirm what data types are in scope (e.g., PHI-adjacent vs PHI) and ensure we do not accept unsupported commitments.

2) **Audit rights / onsite audits**
- Default to evidence pack + third-party reports.
- Onsite audits or bespoke audits require Legal + Security approval and commercial coverage.

3) **Incident response timelines and notification**
- Ensure incident notification language aligns with our operational process.
- Do not accept aggressive timelines without Security review.

4) **Subprocessors and infrastructure disclosures**
- Legal owns; Security supports with accurate lists and evidence pack references.

5) **SLA + service credits**
- Ensure SLA terms align to the selected deployment model (Dedicated vs Private VPC vs on-prem).
- On-prem often needs different language due to shared operational control.

## When to involve Legal / Security (routing rules)
### Engage Legal (Michael Grant / Sofia Mendes) when:
- Any non-standard MSA/DPA language is requested.
- Customer asks for liability changes, indemnities, audit rights, or bespoke confidentiality clauses.
- Data residency becomes a contractual commitment.
- The order form includes custom support obligations, response SLAs, or guaranteed evidence delivery dates.

### Engage Security (Dr. Aisha Rahman / Naomi Feldman / Olga Petrov) when:
- A customer asks for SOC2/ISO reports or any restricted evidence artifacts (Security controls distribution).
- There are detailed requirements around encryption/KMS/HSM, audit logging schemas, or retention guarantees.
- The customer requests completion of a security questionnaire with contractual attachment.
- Any marketing/sales claim risks over-stating posture (e.g., “HIPAA-ready” phrasing).

### Engage Product (Vivek Kulkarni / Lauren Bishop) when:
- Requests imply product gaps or roadmap commitments (e.g., new export formats, novel retention policies, SAML/SCIM edge cases).
- The deal requires a formal exception to standard packaging (e.g., Private without Enhanced support).

## Approvals and deal hygiene (required)
For regulated opportunities (Dedicated or Private), the following must be recorded in the CRM deal notes before final quote:
- Deployment model (Dedicated / Private VPC / on-prem)
- Data types in scope (e.g., “PHI-adjacent,” “PII,” “PCI-related metadata”)
- Required compliance artifacts (evidence pack items requested)
- Retention/residency constraints and whether they are contractual
- Support tier selected and why
- Any promised timelines for questionnaires/evidence

**Approval minimums (internal):**
- Any compliance add-on or non-standard retention commitment: Security + Legal sign-off.
- Any Private/on-prem deal: SE scoping + Product alignment + Legal review before the first redline cycle.

## Internal talk track (how to position packaging)
Use consistent framing:
- “Redwood is optimized for production inference economics with deployment flexibility (Hosted, Dedicated, Private). For regulated workloads, we align commercial packaging to the operational and compliance scope you need—support, evidence handling, and deployment boundaries—so commitments are clear and durable.”

Avoid:
- “We are certified for your specific regulation” (unless explicitly verified and approved).
- Absolute statements about “no data retention anywhere” or “we never have access to decrypted data” without Security-approved language.
