Marketplace RevRec & Invoicing Notes (AWS + GCP)

## Purpose
This page documents Redwood Inference’s revenue recognition and invoicing implications for AWS Marketplace and Google Cloud Marketplace (GCP Marketplace) transactions, including:
- How we recognize revenue for usage-based and contract-based marketplace purchases
- How marketplace fees (revenue share) are recorded
- How refunds/credits are handled (and what we can/can’t do within each marketplace)
- How to reconcile marketplace reporting to Redwood internal billing/metering

This is an internal Finance + Legal reference intended to be consistent with ASC 606 / IFRS 15 principles. It is not customer-facing.

## Scope
In scope:
- Hosted API (usage-based)
- Dedicated (reserved capacity contracts) when transacted via marketplace (where supported)
- Private (platform fee + support) when transacted via marketplace (typically via private offers)
- Compliance / support add-ons sold as separate dimensions/SKUs

Out of scope:
- Direct (non-marketplace) contracts and invoicing (see standard Order Form + invoicing runbook)
- Technical metering implementation details (see Billing Catalog + Marketplace Metering docs)

## Definitions and key objects
- Marketplace “Seller of Record”: AWS/GCP is the billing party to the end customer; Redwood receives net proceeds.
- Marketplace “Private Offer”: negotiated pricing/terms within marketplace tooling; acceptance and billing occurs through AWS/GCP.
- Entitlement: the marketplace subscription record used to provision/limit Redwood usage.
- Metering dimension: the billable unit reported to AWS/GCP (e.g., tokens, requests, capacity-hours), mapped from Redwood internal meters.
- Net proceeds: amount remitted to Redwood after marketplace revenue share and (where applicable) taxes handled by marketplace.

## Accounting policy overview
### Principal vs agent (gross vs net)
In most marketplace scenarios Redwood is the primary party delivering the service (we host/operate the inference platform) and AWS/GCP facilitates procurement and billing. However, the end customer is billed by the marketplace and the marketplace collects consideration.

Policy position (default):
- We record revenue on a gross basis for the service delivered to the customer, with marketplace revenue share recorded as a selling expense (or contra-revenue only if required by local statutory guidance). 
- We record Accounts Receivable against the marketplace (not the end customer) to the extent a receivable exists based on reports/remittance timing.

If a specific private offer or marketplace program introduces conditions that materially change control indicators (e.g., marketplace controls pricing and has primary responsibility for delivery), Finance must re-assess principal/agent for that program/region.

### Revenue recognition timing by purchase type
1) Usage-based (Hosted API) via marketplace
- Performance obligation: stand-ready access to API + actual usage (tokens/embeddings/reranks).
- Recognition: as usage occurs (typically daily), based on Redwood internal metering as the system of record, reconciled to marketplace metering reports.
- Cutoff: revenue accrued through period-end for delivered usage regardless of when marketplace remits cash.

2) Committed spend / contract dimensions (where offered)
- If structured as a committed spend with consumption over time:
  - Recognize revenue as usage occurs up to the committed amount.
  - Unused committed amount: generally deferred and recognized over the contractual period if stand-ready access is the performance obligation, subject to breakage assessment.
- If the marketplace product is a fixed subscription for a term (e.g., “Platform fee per month”):
  - Recognize ratably over the subscription term.

3) Dedicated capacity reservations
- Performance obligation: reserved capacity (stand-ready) and related platform access.
- Recognition: ratably over the reservation term (monthly straight-line), unless the contract specifically ties consideration to delivered capacity-hours and metering supports it.
- Overages (burst capacity) if separately metered: recognize as used.

4) Private deployments (Private / VPC / on-prem package)
Marketplace structures vary; common patterns:
- Subscription (platform fee): recognize ratably over term.
- Professional services (if included): recognize as delivered based on hours/milestones; ensure SKU/offer distinguishes services from subscription where required.
- Support add-ons: ratably over term.

### Multiple performance obligations and allocation
If an offer bundles multiple components (e.g., “Private Platform + Support + Compliance Add-on”), Finance must ensure:
- Separate line items/dimensions exist where practical.
- Standalone selling prices (SSP) are documented for allocation when components are bundled.

As a guardrail for marketplace packaging: avoid bundling implementation services into marketplace SKUs unless the marketplace supports clear separation and the legal terms align.

## Marketplace fees and cost treatment
- AWS/GCP revenue share is recorded as a marketplace fee expense (typically within Sales & Marketing or Cost of Revenue depending on policy; Redwood default is Sales & Marketing unless the program is treated as a distribution channel cost directly tied to delivery).
- Record fee based on marketplace statements (not estimates), unless cutoff requires accrual.

Recommended accounts (illustrative; use current COA names in NetSuite):
- Revenue: Marketplace - Hosted Usage / Marketplace - Subscriptions
- Contra/Expense: Marketplace Fees
- Receivable: Due from Marketplace
- Cash: Marketplace Remittances

## Invoicing implications
### Who invoices the customer
- The marketplace invoices the customer. Redwood does not issue an invoice to the customer for marketplace-billed usage/subscriptions.
- Redwood may provide a “billing support letter” or usage summary for customer procurement, but it must not be presented as a tax invoice.

### What we provide to customers
Permitted (on request):
- Usage reports (tokens, requests, model family, environment) from Redwood Console
- Mapping of Redwood usage categories to marketplace dimensions (for customer chargeback)
- Remittance timing expectations and support escalation contacts

Not permitted (without Legal approval):
- Re-issuing marketplace invoices with Redwood branding
- Making commitments about marketplace tax treatment (VAT/GST) beyond directing customers to AWS/GCP invoice documentation

### Purchase Orders (POs) and vendor onboarding
- Customers typically do vendor onboarding with AWS/GCP, not Redwood.
- For Private Offers, customers may request Redwood W-9/W-8 or SOC documentation; coordinate via Security/Compliance and Legal, and clarify that billing is via marketplace.

## Refunds, credits, and adjustments
### General rule
- Refunds/credits to marketplace customers must be processed through the marketplace mechanisms, not through Redwood’s direct invoicing.
- Redwood may provide service credits inside Redwood (e.g., additional usage allowance) only if consistent with:
  - Marketplace policies
  - The customer’s private offer terms
  - Revenue recognition guidance (credits may reduce transaction price)

### Usage-based disputes (Hosted API)
Common scenarios:
- Customer claims unexpected spend due to misconfiguration
- Metering mismatch (Redwood vs marketplace)

Process:
1) Support confirms incident/dispute scope and time window.
2) Billing Engineering validates internal metering and dimension mapping for the subscription entitlement.
3) Finance determines whether an adjustment is warranted.
4) If warranted:
   - Prefer marketplace credit/refund path (AWS/GCP credit memo equivalent), following marketplace seller workflows.
   - If marketplace cannot issue retroactive adjustments for certain dimensions, Legal + Finance decide on an alternative (e.g., issuing a future-period discount via private offer amendment).

Accounting:
- Refunds reduce revenue in the period identified (or as a subsequent event if applicable), with corresponding reduction in receivable from marketplace.

### Subscription cancellations
- Marketplace cancellations generally stop future billing but do not automatically refund past periods.
- For prepaid/annual private offers, refunds depend on private offer terms and marketplace policy.

Accounting:
- If a refund is approved, reverse deferred revenue for the remaining term and reduce receivable/cash accordingly.

### Promotional credits / trials
Policy:
- Avoid marketplace “$0 with credits” structures that are ambiguous for revrec.
- If a marketplace trial is offered:
  - Ensure the trial is clearly separate from paid subscription/usage.
  - Track conversion and start date of paid entitlement.

If Redwood grants credits inside the Redwood platform (non-marketplace):
- Treat as variable consideration / discount, and ensure it is reflected in net revenue where it impacts the transaction price.
- Do not represent internal credits as marketplace credits.

## Reconciliation: marketplace reports vs Redwood internal billing
### Source of truth
- Redwood internal metering is the operational source of truth for usage delivered.
- Marketplace reports are the commercial source of truth for amounts billed/collected.
- Reconciliation is required at least monthly, and weekly during launch/transition periods.

### Minimum reconciliation outputs (monthly close)
For each marketplace (AWS, GCP) and each SKU/dimension:
- Units billed (marketplace) vs units metered (Redwood)
- Gross billed amount (marketplace) vs gross revenue recognized (Redwood)
- Marketplace fees (per statement) vs accrued fees
- Net remittance vs cash received
- Open receivable balance vs “due from marketplace” aging based on remittance schedule

### Recommended workflow
1) Extract Redwood internal usage summary by marketplace entitlement
- Group by: marketplace (AWS/GCP), seller account, entitlement ID, SKU, dimension, day/month

2) Extract marketplace statements
- AWS: metering/billing reports + disbursement/remittance reports
- GCP: marketplace earnings and billing exports (as provided by partner portal)

3) Map dimensions
- Use the approved SKU mapping catalog (v2) as the only mapping reference.
- Any unmapped dimensions or new SKUs must be flagged to Product + Billing (risk of misbilling and incorrect revrec).

4) Investigate exceptions
Common exception categories:
- Entitlement propagation delay (usage occurred before marketplace recognized subscription)
- Dimension mismatch due to SKU rename or legacy listing
- Partial-day proration differences for subscription start/end
- Currency conversion differences (if marketplace bills in local currency but Redwood books in USD)

5) Close and post entries
- Accrue revenue to match delivered usage and/or ratable subscription periods.
- Accrue marketplace fees if statements lag close.

### Exception thresholds and escalation
- >1.0% variance in units or revenue per SKU/month: escalate to Billing + Partnerships and open a tracking ticket.
- Any customer-impacting misbilling: coordinate with Support and Legal before issuing marketplace adjustments.

Owners:
- Finance (RevRec): owns close and final numbers
- Billing Engineering: owns meter/dimension correctness
- Partnerships: owns marketplace portal processes and submissions

## Cutover from legacy listings / SKU changes
During listing refresh and SKU migration:
- Expect overlap where customers are on legacy SKUs while new SKUs are live.
- Finance must ensure:
  - Legacy SKUs remain mapped to internal meters until fully sunset
  - Revenue reporting separates legacy vs v2 SKUs for at least 2 quarters post-launch
  - Any customer migration (private offer amendment or subscription change) is documented for revrec (contract modification assessment)

Contract modification guidance:
- If the change adds distinct services at SSP: treat as separate contract.
- If the change modifies price/term for the same service: account as contract modification with cumulative catch-up or prospective treatment as appropriate.

## Journal entry examples (illustrative)
### Example A: Usage-based month, gross revenue with marketplace fee
Assume:
- Gross usage billed: $100,000
- Marketplace fee: $15,000
- Net remittance expected: $85,000

At month-end (accrual based on usage delivered):
- Dr Due from Marketplace $100,000
- Cr Marketplace Revenue $100,000

When marketplace fee statement is issued (or at month-end if known):
- Dr Marketplace Fees Expense $15,000
- Cr Due from Marketplace $15,000

Upon cash remittance:
- Dr Cash $85,000
- Cr Due from Marketplace $85,000

### Example B: Annual subscription sold via private offer
Assume:
- Annual platform subscription: $240,000
- Start date: Feb 1

At contract start (if billed/recognized via marketplace subscription):
- Dr Due from Marketplace $240,000
- Cr Deferred Revenue $240,000

Monthly recognition:
- Dr Deferred Revenue $20,000
- Cr Subscription Revenue $20,000

## Controls and audit considerations
- Ensure segregation of duties: offer creation (Partnerships) vs revrec policy (Finance) vs system mapping changes (Engineering).
- Maintain evidence for:
  - SKU mapping approvals
  - Contract terms (private offer PDFs/portal exports)
  - Marketplace statements and remittance reports
  - Reconciliation workpapers and variance investigations

Retention:
- Store reconciliation workpapers and marketplace statements in the Finance close folder for the fiscal year (minimum 7 years, or per corporate retention policy).

## Operational checklist (monthly close)
1) Confirm new SKUs/dimensions launched this period; verify mapping exists.
2) Pull Redwood usage by entitlement for the month; validate totals to internal dashboards.
3) Pull AWS/GCP billing + remittance statements.
4) Complete reconciliation and investigate variances above threshold.
5) Post revenue accruals/deferrals and marketplace fee accruals.
6) Tie out Due from Marketplace to expected remittance schedule.
7) Document any refunds/credits approved and confirm they were processed through marketplace.
