GPU Surge Mitigation & Graceful Degradation for Tenant Throughput Guarantees

h1. Overview

Dedicated customers require firm throughput guarantees even under tenant surge scenarios. Without surge controls, a single tenant’s sudden increase in request volume can cause latency spikes or dropped requests for adjacent tenants on shared GPU pools (“noisy neighbor”).

This page documents the current surge mitigation system for Dedicated Capacity pools, including the quota model, enforcement mechanics, eviction policy, autoscaling behavior, and observability requirements. It supersedes earlier “eviction-only” approaches.

h1. Goals and Non-Goals

h2. Goals
* Enforce per-tenant contractual throughput guarantees (hard commitments) during transient and sustained surges.
* Provide deterministic graceful degradation for best-effort tenants under contention.
* Trigger autoscaling quickly enough to restore headroom without excessive flapping.
* Ensure no silent failures: every throttle/eviction/autoscale decision is auditable.

h2. Non-Goals
* This system does not attempt to guarantee absolute latency under global fleet exhaustion; it guarantees committed throughput within the capacity envelope of the Dedicated pool (and triggers autoscaling to expand that envelope).
* This system does not change customer contract definitions; it implements enforcement.

h1. Problem Statement

Current behavior (pre-rollout): when a tenant suddenly increases request volume, shared-pool neighbors experience elevated p95/p99 latency and intermittent 429/503s. In some cases, an eviction-only policy is too coarse: it can produce unnecessary churn and cache thrash, and it does not provide a predictable fairness model for best-effort traffic.

Objective: introduce a multi-tiered surge mitigation system that enforces committed throughput for SLA-backed tenants, gracefully degrades best-effort tenants, and integrates with autoscaling triggers to recover capacity.

h1. Architecture Summary

h2. Hierarchical quota model
We use a hierarchical quota model per tenant:
1) *Committed throughput (hard contract)*: reserved and protected.
2) *Soft quota (best-effort)*: opportunistic usage subject to contention.
3) *Burst reservoir (time-limited credits)*: allows short bursts while bounding long-tail abuse.

h2. Enforcement point
Enforcement occurs at the *lane scheduler* level (not per-GPU process) to preserve kernel-level batching efficiency and avoid per-process oscillations.

h2. Control actions
* *Cooperative throttling (preferred)*: token-bucket backpressure and explicit overload responses (429/503 with Retry-After) for best-effort lanes.
* *Eviction (last resort)*: reserved for persistent contract violations or pathological behavior (e.g., sustained over-commit + queue blowup) after drain attempts.

h1. Key Design Decisions

1) *Lane-level enforcement over per-process enforcement*
* Preserves batching and reduces GPU underutilization during throttling.

2) *Token-bucket + credit decay*
* Token-bucket accounting provides deterministic throttling.
* Credit decay bounds prolonged bursts and reduces “slow drain” abuse.

3) *Autoscaling: reactive fast-path + predictive scale-down*
* Fast-path scale-up is triggered during sustained overload.
* Scale-down uses a 5-minute rolling trend + hysteresis to avoid flapping.

4) *Telemetry is part of the feature*
* The system is considered incomplete without end-to-end metrics, audit logs, and operator dashboards.

h1. Configuration

h2. Per-tenant settings (Console)
| Setting | Description | Default |
| committed_qps | SLA-backed committed throughput | (per contract) |
| burst_window_seconds | Burst window for credits | 20s |
| burst_credits_multiplier | Burst credits relative to baseline | 2x |
| autoscale_priority | Tenant influence on scale urgency | normal |

Notes:
* Defaults were tightened from earlier prototypes to reduce short-term cost spikes under coordinated surges.
* RBAC: only authorized operators can modify committed throughput and autoscale priority.

h1. Implementation Notes (Serving + Scheduler)

* *Reservation table*: scheduler maintains a tenant-aware reservation table with per-lane token accounting and 1s-granularity credit decay.
* *Batching + KV cache*: when a tenant is throttled, we preserve KV cache entries where possible to avoid warm-up penalties and reduce collateral damage to unthrottled tenants.
* *Overload responses*: best-effort lanes receive explicit backpressure (429/503 + Retry-After) rather than implicit queue growth.

h1. Eviction Policy

Eviction is used only after cooperative throttling fails to restore stability.

Eviction flow:
1) *Soft-quiesce*: mark processes for drain.
2) *Graceful draining*: attempt drain for up to 15s (increased from earlier 10s after observing false-positive hard evictions during large-batch completions).
3) *Hard-evict*: if still over quota / destabilizing, terminate and emit detailed reason codes.

Safeguards:
* Epoch-based lease checks prevent a race where soft-quiesce overlaps with autoscale reconciliation.

h1. Autoscaling Behavior

h2. Triggers
* Reactive scale-up fires within *45s* of sustained overload (updated from earlier 60s target based on canary results and improved signal quality).
* Autoscale decisions are annotated with the dominant signal (e.g., committed throughput deficit vs queue depth vs GPU saturation).

h2. Provisioning targets
* Fast-path provisioning targets: <60s for common GPU types in pre-warmed workflows.
* Cold-path provisioning may still exceed 2 minutes depending on cluster state; this is explicitly surfaced in decision traces.

h2. Cost controls
* Autoscaling respects reserved capacity commitments and enforces per-pool scale ceilings.

h1. Acceptance Criteria (Current)

1) *SLA protection*: During synthetic surge tests (50x baseline QPS for 30s), SLA-backed tenants maintain >=95% of committed throughput and p95 latency stays within 1.5x baseline.
2) *Deterministic degradation*: Best-effort tenants are throttled deterministically via token-bucket backpressure; SLA tenants do not experience downstream latency degradation attributable to best-effort spikes.
3) *Autoscale responsiveness*: Scale-up triggers within 45s of sustained overload; capacity increases restore baseline throughput within defined hysteresis windows.
4) *No silent failures*: All throttles, evictions, and autoscale events are recorded in Console and exposed via per-tenant audit logs and metrics.
5) *Safe rollout*: End-to-end canary rollout with on-call guardrails completed; rollback playbook validated in staging and exercised during canary.
6) *Secure configuration*: All knobs documented in Console with RBAC; defaults are conservative and reviewed with PM/SRE.

h1. Benchmarks & Results

Staging and early-production results (mixed workload):
* *Staging synthetic test*: 100-tenant mixed workload, one tenant surging to ~60x baseline for 45s. Committed tenants maintained ~97–98% throughput; median p95 latency multiple ~1.3x baseline.
* *Eviction test*: Two persistent violators configured. Soft-quiesce succeeded ~90% of the time without hard eviction after increasing drain window to 15s; hard eviction restored headroom in ~10–15s when needed.
* *Autoscale latency*: A100 pools with fast-path provisioning: median ~58–62s in pre-warmed workflow; cold-path remains ~120–160s.
* *Cost delta*: Tightened burst defaults reduced short-term GPU usage impact; net increase under heavy synthetic surges ~3–4% (down from earlier ~6%), with predictive scale-down recovering within ~10–15 minutes depending on workload.

h2. Alerts
* Page if any SLA tenant loses >10% of committed throughput for >30s.
* Page if autoscaler fails 3 consecutive provisioning attempts.
* Warn if false-positive eviction rate exceeds 1% (weekly rolling).

h2. Dashboards and audit logs
* Per-pool and per-tenant dashboards in Console.
* Every throttle/eviction/autoscale event emits an annotated audit log with correlation_id and responsible_policy.

h1. Test Plan

* Unit tests: token-bucket accounting + decay logic.
* Integration tests: scheduler-level enforcement preserves batching across lanes.
* Chaos tests: flapping tenants and partial network partitions; validate graceful degradation and no silent data loss.
* Performance regressions: p50/p95/p99 pre/post on mixed workloads.
* Canary: dedicated-staging with traffic mirroring for an enterprise customer cluster before broad rollout.

h1. Rollout Status and Plan

* Phase 1: Feature flags behind pool-level opt-in; deployed to staging. (complete)
* Phase 2: Enabled for low-risk Dedicated pools; 48h soak with synthetic surges. (complete)
* Phase 3: Controlled canary for two enterprise customers with on-call monitoring and quick rollback. (complete)
* Phase 4: Enable-by-default for Dedicated pools; deprecate eviction-only policy after 4 weeks of steady telemetry. (in progress)

Rollback:
* Immediate disable of token accounting and autoscale fast-path if canary shows regressions.
* Revert PR is maintained alongside the rollout changes.
