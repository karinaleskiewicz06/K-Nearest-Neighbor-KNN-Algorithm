Emergency Change Policy (Production)

h1. Summary
This policy defines what qualifies as an *Emergency Change* and the minimum approvals and documentation required to execute it. Emergency changes are allowed when needed to restore or protect production availability or customer data.

h1. Definition
An Emergency Change is a production-impacting change executed outside the normal change window or expedited due to an active incident. Examples include:
- Region failover / traffic cutover
- Disabling a feature flag to stop an outage
- Increasing rate limits or reducing request capacity to protect core systems

h1. Minimum requirements (must do)
During the incident (real-time):
1) Record the change in the incident channel:
   - Who executed
   - What was changed
   - Why
   - Time
   - Rollback plan
2) Obtain a second-person sign-off (can be via Slack thread) from one of:
   - SRE oncall
   - Infra oncall
   - Platform oncall
3) Ensure customer communications are coordinated with the IC (or Comms lead).

After the incident (within 24 hours):
1) Create or update the incident review with:
   - Exact commands/config toggles
   - Timeline
   - Validation results
   - Any customer impact
2) If a permanent configuration change was made using emergency tooling, open a follow-up to backport it to IaC (Terraform) so we return to auditable desired state.

h1. Prohibited emergency changes
The following are not allowed under emergency policy except with Security oncall approval:
- Disabling audit logging
- Broadening RBAC permissions beyond the minimum required
- Changing key management / encryption settings

h1. Notes
- If you are unsure whether something is an emergency change, treat it as one and document it.
- This policy is intentionally lightweight; the priority during an incident is safe restoration of service with clear rollback.
