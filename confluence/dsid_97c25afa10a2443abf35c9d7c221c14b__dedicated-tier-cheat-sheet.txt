Dedicated Tiers + Throughput SLO — Seller Cheat Sheet (v1)

## Purpose
This page is the seller-facing summary of Redwood Dedicated tiers (Standard / Plus / Premium): what we guarantee, how to position each tier, how throughput is measured, common objections + talk tracks, and quoting/discount guardrails.

**Audience:** AEs, SEs, Deal Desk, Sales Ops, CS.

---

## TL;DR (what to say in one minute)
- **Dedicated** = reserved GPU capacity + isolation + predictable performance, with an **explicit throughput floor** (tier-dependent) and a defined escalation/credit process.
- **Standard** fits steady, cost-sensitive workloads where brief dips are acceptable and the customer can tolerate best-effort failover.
- **Plus** is for production workloads that need **higher headroom + defined burst** and faster support/triage.
- **Premium** is for enterprise/regulated or high-revenue workloads that need the **strongest performance posture**, warm-spare capacity, and the strictest support response.

## What is actually guaranteed (seller-safe language)
### The Dedicated throughput commitment (floor)
Redwood commits that a Dedicated deployment will meet or exceed a **minimum sustained throughput floor** for the purchased tier/shape **when operated within documented constraints**.

**Plain English:** If the customer sends traffic that matches the assumptions we sized for (model/version, context, streaming mode, etc.), the deployment will sustain at least the committed throughput; if not, Redwood will triage and remediate, and may issue credits per the Order Form.

### How throughput is measured (high-level)
This is the simplified description suitable for customers. (Engineering details live in the SLO definition doc.)
- **Metric:** Output token throughput measured at the Redwood serving layer for the Dedicated deployment (tokens/sec), attributed to the customer’s deployment.
- **Window:** Evaluated over rolling windows to avoid penalizing brief jitter (exact windows/percentiles are defined in the Order Form / SLO exhibit).
- **Applies to:** The **pinned model + version** and the **approved serving configuration** for that deployment.

### Sizing constraints (what must be true for the commitment to apply)
The commitment assumes:
- The customer uses a **supported model/version** for the purchased shape.
- Requests stay within **documented max context** and recommended batch/parallelism settings.
- No customer-side behavior that artificially depresses throughput (see exclusions).

## Exclusions (what we do NOT commit against)
Use this list when procurement asks “what are the carve-outs?”

Throughput commitment does **not** apply to:
- **Customer-side throttling or backpressure** (e.g., client timeouts causing retries, premature disconnects, or intentionally low concurrency).
- **Unsupported model/version** or unapproved model switches (including experimental forks) on the same deployment.
- Requests that exceed **max supported context length** for the shape/model, or exceed configured limits (tokens, request size).
- **Misuse patterns** that degrade performance: pathological prompts, adversarial sampling settings, excessive tool/function recursion, or sustained traffic that violates the agreed traffic envelope.
- Periods of **planned maintenance** within communicated windows.
- Customer-managed components outside Redwood’s control (e.g., customer VPC network issues for Private/VPC variants).

## Burst policy (how to talk about it)
**Definition:** “Burst” is throughput above the committed floor when spare capacity exists and the deployment’s tier allows it.

Seller-safe statements:
- Burst is **additive** and **not required** for SLO compliance.
- Burst availability depends on region/shape supply and current fleet conditions.
- Burst usage may be **metered and billed** depending on the customer’s package (confirm SKU).

Common customer question: “Can you guarantee 2x during spikes?”
- Standard answer: “We can offer a committed floor plus a tiered burst posture. If you need a guaranteed peak, we should size the committed throughput to the peak or add additional deployments.”

## Picking the right tier (discovery checklist)
### Qualifying questions (ask these early)
1) **Revenue impact / criticality:** What happens if throughput drops 20% for 30–60 minutes?
2) **Traffic shape:** Steady vs spiky? Time-of-day peaks?
3) **Latency sensitivity:** Is the priority tokens/sec, p95 latency, or both?
4) **Model constraints:** Single pinned model or multi-model? Any planned model switches?
5) **Context lengths:** Typical and worst-case input + output lengths.
6) **Availability requirements:** Is failover required? Single region acceptable?
7) **Procurement posture:** Do they require written SLOs, credits, response times?

### Rule of thumb
- Choose **Standard** when: cost dominates, steady demand, customer can handle “best effort” burst and failover.
- Choose **Plus** when: production workload + spikes + needs defined operational posture.
- Choose **Premium** when: enterprise/legal requires strongest commitments, or downtime/perf regression creates significant business risk.

## Shapes & pricing (how to talk about dollars without over-committing)
### Pricing is anchored to standardized shapes
Dedicated pricing maps **tier → approved shape(s) → region**.

Seller guidance:
- Don’t quote “per GPU” pricing. Quote the **shape SKU** (capacity bundle) + tier.
- If the customer wants a non-standard shape, treat as **exception** (Deal Desk + Infra sign-off).

### List price bands (for early-stage qualification only)
**These are directional ranges.** Use CPQ for an official quote.
- **Standard:** typically **$20k–$70k / month** depending on shape + region
- **Plus:** typically **$35k–$110k / month** depending on shape + region
- **Premium:** typically **$60k–$200k+ / month** depending on shape + region and HA posture

What changes price most:
- GPU type (e.g., L40S vs A100 vs H100), GPU count, region supply, and HA/warm-spare posture.

## Common objections + talk tracks
### Objection: “Hosted is cheaper. Why pay for Dedicated?”
**Talk track:** “Hosted optimizes for flexibility and shared fleet efficiency. Dedicated buys isolation and predictability: reserved capacity, clear throughput floor, and a defined escalation/credit path. If performance and cost predictability matter for production, Dedicated is the right tool.”

### Objection: “Tokens/sec varies by prompt. Your SLO seems squishy.”
**Talk track:** “Correct—raw throughput varies by context length and sampling. That’s why the commitment is tied to supported models/configs and measured over rolling windows. We size the deployment with you against representative traffic, and we provide dashboards so you can validate performance.”

### Objection: “We need to switch models frequently.”
**Talk track:** “Dedicated commitments apply to the pinned model/version. If you need frequent switching, we can discuss either (a) multiple dedicated deployments, (b) a staged rollout plan, or (c) using Hosted for experimentation and Dedicated for steady-state production.”

### Objection: “What happens if you miss the commitment?”
**Talk track:** “We treat sustained misses as an operational incident: triage, mitigation, and a documented postmortem path. Remedies are defined in the Order Form (typically service credits) depending on severity and duration.”

### Objection: “We require guaranteed failover across regions.”
**Talk track:** “We can support stronger failover posture in Premium with an explicit multi-region design, but it’s an add-on and depends on regional shape availability. Let’s pull in Solutions Engineering to propose an architecture and confirm supply.”

## What NOT to promise (avoid creating contract risk)
Do not promise:
- “Guaranteed p95 latency” unless explicitly included in the Order Form
- “Guaranteed peak/burst to X tokens/sec” (unless committed as a floor)
- “Any model, any time” on a Dedicated deployment (commitment is model/version constrained)
- “Instant provisioning” for supply-constrained shapes/regions
- “Automatic free cross-region failover” without Premium + explicit design/SKU

Safe alternative phrasing:
- “We can commit to a throughput floor under defined operating conditions, and we can size to your peak if you need peak guaranteed.”

## FAQ (seller-facing)
**Q: Is the throughput commitment per deployment or per customer account?**
A: Per **Dedicated deployment** (the reserved capacity unit), tied to the purchased tier/shape.

**Q: Is throughput measured on input tokens, output tokens, or both?**
A: Customer-facing: we describe it as output token throughput. Engineering reporting may include additional internal metrics. Use the SLO exhibit as the source of truth.

**Q: Do retries count against throughput?**
A: Retries can affect observed performance and are often excluded if caused by customer-side timeouts/backpressure. If it becomes a point of contention, escalate.

**Q: Can we offer a POC on Plus and then upgrade to Premium?**
A: Yes. Position as a staged rollout: validate sizing + metrics on Plus, upgrade if operational posture and procurement require it.
