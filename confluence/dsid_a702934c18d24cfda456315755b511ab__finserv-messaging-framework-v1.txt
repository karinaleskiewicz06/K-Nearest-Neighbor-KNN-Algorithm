Financial Services Messaging Framework (v1) — Regulated Workloads ABM Pilot

## Purpose
This framework standardizes Redwood Inference messaging for **financial services (FS)** buyers during the Regulated Workloads ABM Pilot. It is designed to be used in outbound sequences, discovery calls, demos, security reviews, and procurement conversations.

**Primary goal:** Generate qualified pipeline for **Redwood Dedicated** and **Redwood Private (VPC/on-prem)** where compliance, auditability, and predictable performance are buying criteria.

**Secondary goal:** Keep claims compliant and consistent across Marketing, Sales, SE, and Security.

---

## Who this is for (personas)
Below are the primary FS personas we target. In practice, deals require multi-threading; use the “who cares about what” table to tailor talk tracks.

### Persona 1: CISO / Security Engineering Leader
**Typical titles:** CISO, VP Security, Director of Security Engineering, IAM Lead

**What they care about:**
- Auditability (who did what, when), incident response readiness, security controls, vendor risk
- Encryption and key management boundaries (KMS/HSM), access controls, SSO/SAML
- Data handling: retention, residency, and vendor policy clarity

**Common questions:**
- “Do you store prompts/outputs? For how long? Can we control retention?”
- “Can you run this in our VPC or on-prem?”
- “Do you support SSO/SAML and role-based access? Do you have audit logs?”

### Persona 2: Platform Engineering / ML Engineering / GenAI Platform Owner
**Typical titles:** Head of Platform, ML Platform Lead, Staff Engineer (AI), Director of Engineering

**What they care about:**
- Predictable throughput and latency under load; performance cost tradeoffs
- Deployment topology and integration: VPC networking, private connectivity, observability
- Operational tooling: rollouts, fallback policies, per-route dashboards, tracing hooks

**Common questions:**
- “Can you hit our latency targets for chat + retrieval workloads?”
- “How do we isolate environments and tenants?”
- “How do we manage model versioning, canaries, and rollback?”

### Persona 3: Risk / Compliance / Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM)
**Typical titles:** VP Risk, Compliance Officer, TPRM Analyst, Operational Risk Manager

**What they care about:**
- Evidence (SOC 2/ISO artifacts process), policy clarity, contractual commitments
- Data processing boundaries, sub-processors, audit rights, incident notification

**Common questions:**
- “What’s the SOC 2 scope and how do we review it?”
- “Where is data processed? Can we constrain regions?”
- “Can we get standard security questionnaire responses quickly?”

### Persona 4 (supporting): Procurement / Legal
**Typical titles:** Procurement Manager, Vendor Manager, Counsel

**What they care about:**
- Pricing predictability, commercial terms, liability, SLA language, DPA

## Positioning (FS-specific)
### Core positioning statement
**Redwood Inference helps financial institutions run LLM inference with predictable performance and production-grade controls across hosted, dedicated, and private deployments—so you can meet security/compliance requirements without sacrificing latency, reliability, or unit economics.**

### Why now (FS trigger events)
Use these triggers to personalize outbound:
- Security teams restricting use of public LLM APIs; internal mandate to “bring inference inside the boundary”
- Existing inference endpoints failing latency/throughput SLOs (fraud ops, support, analyst copilots)
- Vendor risk/TPRM blocking pilots due to weak auditability, unclear retention, or lack of enterprise controls
- Cost overrun from token spend; need for batching/caching/routing levers and predictable capacity

## Typical FS use cases (approved examples)
Use cases should be framed as **“customer-controlled data + governance”** and **“workload-appropriate deployment.”**

1) **Fraud operations assistant**
- Summarize case notes, draft investigator narratives, explain alerts
- Constraints: strict audit trail, role-based access, predictable latency during peak events

2) **Customer support and call summarization**
- Summaries, next-best-action suggestions, knowledge base Q&A
- Constraints: retention controls, access logging, data residency requirements for some regions

3) **AML/KYC analyst copilot (assistive workflows)**
- Summarize documentation, draft review notes, search policy corpora
- Constraints: governance workflows, “human-in-the-loop,” auditability, TPRM scrutiny

4) **Internal policy / procedure Q&A**
- Retrieval-augmented generation on internal docs
- Constraints: private connectivity, logging, and change management for model versions

## Redwood differentiators that resonate in FS
### Pillar A: Deployment flexibility to meet governance requirements
- **Redwood Dedicated:** dedicated GPU pools for predictable throughput and data isolation
- **Redwood Private:** deploy in a customer VPC and (where applicable) on-prem for stricter boundary requirements

**FS translation:** “You don’t have to choose between innovation and your security perimeter. Redwood meets you where your risk posture is.”

### Pillar B: Auditability and enterprise access controls
- Audit logging capabilities and export patterns (see evidence pack index)
- RBAC and enterprise identity integrations (SSO/SAML where supported by deployment mode)

**FS translation:** “Security teams get evidence and traceability; engineers get an API and runtime that can actually operate at scale.”

### Pillar C: Predictable performance + cost controls (production economics)
- High-throughput runtime with workload-aware batching/caching
- Smart routing and fallback policies to keep apps online when capacity is constrained
- Console-level visibility into latency and cost drivers

**FS translation:** “Stable latency under load and control over cost per interaction—without building a bespoke serving stack.”

## Product mapping (what to recommend when)
Use this section to quickly map requirements to the right deployment.

### If the buyer requires strong network isolation / private connectivity
Recommend: **Redwood Private (VPC)**
- Common triggers: internal policy prohibits external SaaS endpoints for sensitive workloads; strict network egress rules

### If the buyer needs predictable throughput, but not full private control plane
Recommend: **Redwood Dedicated**
- Common triggers: performance SLOs, consistent throughput, predictable cost, isolation from multitenant noisy neighbors

### If the buyer is early-stage and needs fastest time-to-value
Recommend: **Hosted API** (with clear guardrails)
- Use for prototyping and low-risk workloads; do not position Hosted as the answer to strict “inside our boundary” mandates

**Internal guidance:** In FS ABM, default your CTA toward **Dedicated or Private discovery** unless the account explicitly lacks regulated constraints.

## Approved claims (safe language)
Use the following language in customer-facing materials. If you need to go beyond these, loop in Security (Dr. Aisha Rahman / Naomi Feldman) before sending externally.

### Security & compliance (approved)
- “Redwood supports **Dedicated** and **Private (VPC / on-prem options)** for customers that need stronger isolation and control.”
- “We provide **audit logs** for platform and inference operations and can share sample fields and export patterns under NDA.”
- “We support **encryption in transit and at rest** and can integrate with customer key management approaches depending on deployment mode.”
- “We have a standard process for sharing compliance evidence (e.g., SOC 2) and security documentation under appropriate confidentiality terms.”
- “We support enterprise access controls (RBAC) and identity integrations (SSO/SAML where applicable).”

### Performance & reliability (approved)
- “Redwood is optimized for **production-grade inference**: throughput, latency, and reliability.”
- “Dedicated capacity can be configured for predictable throughput and scaled based on agreed policies.”
- “We provide console-level visibility into latency and cost drivers, with tools to manage rollouts and fallback policies.”

### What we do NOT say (guardrails)
Do not use these phrases without explicit Legal/Security approval and documented scope:
- “HIPAA-ready,” “PCI-compliant,” “guaranteed data residency,” “guaranteed zero data retention,” “fully air-gapped”
- “We never store any customer data” (use precise retention language from the Security FAQ)
- “SOC 2 certified” (use the correct phrasing from the evidence process)

## Objection handling (finserv-specific)
### Objection: “We can’t send sensitive data to a SaaS API.”
**Response:**
- “That’s common in FS. We support **Private (VPC)** and (where applicable) **on-prem** options so inference can run within your network boundary. We can scope a short architecture review to confirm connectivity, logging, and key management requirements.”

**Follow-up question:**
- “Is your constraint primarily network isolation, key control, or retention/residency?”

### Objection: “Our security team needs audit logs and evidence before they’ll allow a pilot.”
**Response:**
- “Understood—FS teams usually want auditability early. We have a **regulated workloads security FAQ** and an **evidence pack index** that lays out what we can share under NDA. We can also walk through audit log fields and export patterns in a 30-minute technical security review.”

### Objection: “We need predictable performance; we’ve seen latency spikes with other endpoints.”
**Response:**
- “This is exactly why Dedicated exists: predictable throughput, configurable scaling policies, and isolation. On top of that, Redwood’s runtime is designed for production inference (batching/caching and routing policies) and the Console exposes token-level cost/latency breakdowns.”

**Follow-up question:**
- “What are your P95/P99 targets and concurrency profile? Is the workload bursty (fraud events) or steady-state?”

### Objection: “We’re worried about vendor lock-in / model churn.”
**Response:**
- “Redwood’s value is in serving: predictable performance, deployment flexibility, and operational tooling. We can pin model versions, run canaries, and set fallback policies—so you can evolve models without breaking production.”

### Objection: “We can build this ourselves.”
**Response:**
- “Many teams can build an endpoint. The harder part is production operations: consistent latency under load, capacity planning, auditability, rollouts, incident response, and cost controls. Redwood provides a unified runtime and operations layer across hosted, dedicated, and private so you don’t have to maintain the full stack.”

## Discovery questions (recommended)
Use these early to qualify for Dedicated/Private and to shape the security review.

### Security / governance
1) “What policies govern whether LLM inference can run outside your network boundary?”
2) “Do you require audit logs for inference access and admin operations? What fields do you need?”
3) “What are your retention expectations for prompts/outputs and logs?”
4) “Do you need customer-managed keys (KMS/HSM) or specific key custody requirements?”

### Performance / operations
5) “What are your latency targets (P95/P99) and concurrency? Is it interactive or batch?”
6) “How do you plan to handle model versioning, evaluation, and rollback?”
7) “What does ‘success’ look like for a 2–3 week pilot (benchmarks, architecture validation, security signoff)?”

### Commercial / procurement
8) “Do you expect to buy via cloud marketplace or direct? Any vendor onboarding timelines we should plan for?”
9) “Is predictable capacity a hard requirement, or are you optimizing for cost first?”
