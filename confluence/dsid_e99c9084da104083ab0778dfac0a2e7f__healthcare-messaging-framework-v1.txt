Healthcare Messaging Framework (v1) — PHI-adjacent & regulated workloads

## Purpose
This messaging framework is the approved, compliance-reviewed source of truth for positioning Redwood Inference to healthcare prospects with PHI-adjacent or regulated workloads. It is designed for the Vertical ABM pilot and for sales-led discovery, security reviews, and pilot/POC scoping.

**What this is:** persona pains, positioning pillars, approved claims, objection handling, and use-case talk tracks specific to healthcare.

**What this is not:** legal advice or a promise of compliance certification. Use the security FAQ and evidence pack for substantiation.

---

## Definitions (use consistently)
**PHI-adjacent workload:** A workload that *touches* clinical workflows, patient/member communications, or billing/coding context, but may or may not require direct PHI processing (e.g., patient support content with de-identified inputs).

**PHI workload:** A workload where protected health information may be processed, stored, or logged.

**Regulated healthcare buyer:** Typically includes HIPAA-covered entities and business associates in the US; additional privacy/security regimes may apply (state privacy laws, contractual security addenda, internal risk controls).

**Redwood Inference provides production-grade LLM inference with deployment flexibility (hosted, dedicated, private) and operational controls (auditability, access controls, retention/residency levers) that healthcare teams need to ship regulated workloads with predictable latency and cost.**

2) **Production reliability + predictable performance**

3) **Economics without sacrificing quality**
- Batching/caching and cost controls with visibility
- Model/version pinning and rollout controls to prevent regressions

## Deployment guidance (what to recommend when)
Use this section to align the “right” Redwood deployment mode with PHI risk and procurement constraints.

### Recommended mapping (high-level)
**Hosted API**
- Best for: non-PHI or de-identified workloads; prototyping; low friction evaluation
- Typical buyers: innovation teams, product teams that need speed
- Key message: fastest path to benchmarks and value; strong performance + developer experience

**Dedicated (reserved capacity)**
- Best for: regulated workloads needing **capacity isolation** and predictable SLOs; some customers’ security requirements without full private control plane
- Typical buyers: platform teams scaling to production; teams with strict latency/throughput requirements
- Key message: predictable throughput and isolation + Redwood operational tooling

**Private (VPC / on-prem)**
- Best for: PHI workloads or strict network/data boundary requirements; customers requiring private connectivity, customer-managed networking controls, or on-prem constraints
- Typical buyers: health systems, payers, and billing orgs with high security review bar
- Key message: deploy Redwood’s inference platform within your security perimeter; maintain consistent runtime + operations

### Guidance for PHI conversations (do this every time)
- Ask early: **“Will PHI be included in prompts, outputs, logs, or stored datasets?”**
- If *yes or uncertain*, default positioning toward **Private** (VPC / on-prem) until Security confirms requirements.
- If *no (de-identified)*, you can lead with **Hosted** or **Dedicated** depending on SLO and isolation needs.

## Compliance + security requirements (translate into Redwood-friendly language)
Use these as “requirements we commonly see” and map to Redwood capabilities + evidence artifacts.

Common healthcare requirements:
- **Access control:** RBAC; SSO/SAML; user provisioning/deprovisioning expectations (e.g., SCIM)
- **Auditability:** immutable audit trails for admin actions and data access patterns; export for SIEM
- **Encryption:** encryption in transit; encryption at rest; customer key control requirements (KMS/HSM patterns)
- **Retention controls:** ability to define retention windows for logs and stored artifacts; deletion workflows
- **Residency:** constraints around region/VPC boundaries and where data transits
- **Vendor risk:** SOC 2/ISO posture, incident response practices, subprocessors transparency

Approved phrasing (use verbatim where possible):
- “Redwood supports the security controls commonly required for regulated workloads, including encryption, role-based access controls, audit logging, and private deployment options.”
- “We can share our security FAQ and an evidence pack index under NDA to support your security review.”
- “We’ll align deployment mode (Hosted, Dedicated, or Private) to your data classification and network boundary requirements.”

**Do not over-claim:**
- Avoid implying a certification or guarantee of compliance (see ‘Do not say’ list).

## Retention & residency considerations (healthcare-specific talk track)
Healthcare prospects often conflate “data retention” across multiple layers. Use this structure:

1) **Request/response content**
- Clarify whether content is stored, cached, or logged and under what controls.
- Emphasize data minimization: “We encourage sending only what’s required; de-identify when possible.”

2) **Operational logs / audit logs**
- Explain the purpose: accountability and security monitoring.
- Confirm export patterns: “Audit logs can be exported to your logging platform/SIEM depending on deployment.”

3) **Regional / network boundaries**
- Ask: “Do you require data to stay within a specific cloud region or within your VPC/on-prem environment?”
- Position Private for strict boundaries.

Suggested discovery questions:
- “What retention window do you require for application logs vs security audit logs?”
- “Do you require customer-managed keys or HSM-backed key custody?”
- “Do you have a policy restricting PHI from leaving your environment?”

## Example use cases (healthcare) + guardrails
Use these examples to anchor the conversation; always pair with guardrails to demonstrate responsible posture.

### Use case A: Member/patient support assistant (chat + routing)
Examples:
- Benefits questions, appointment FAQs, coverage explanations
- Escalation to human agent with summarized context

Where Redwood fits:
- Low-latency chat inference with routing/fallback
- Cost controls for high-volume traffic

Guardrails to state:
- Start with **de-identified** inputs where possible.
- Avoid generating medical advice; include policy-based disclaimers and escalation paths.
- Define a “no-PII/PHI” boundary for Hosted; use Private if PHI is in scope.

Suggested success metrics:
- P95 latency target for first token + steady-state streaming
- Deflection rate with human escalation safety checks

### Use case B: Clinical note / chart summarization (clinician productivity)
Examples:
- Summarize longitudinal notes for review
- Highlight medications/problems with structured output

Where Redwood fits:
- Private deployment patterns for strict data boundaries
- Version pinning and controlled rollouts to prevent quality regressions

Guardrails to state:
- Human-in-the-loop review for any clinical-facing output.
- Use structured outputs to reduce hallucinated formatting.
- Maintain a prompt/eval set and monitor regressions during rollouts.

Suggested discovery questions:
- “What’s the source system and what PHI fields are included?”
- “Do you require on-prem or VPC-only processing?”

### Use case C: Coding assistance / revenue cycle (ICD/CPT suggestions)
Examples:
- Draft coding suggestions from documentation
- Summarize claim context for back-office workflows

Where Redwood fits:
- Batch throughput optimization for back-office processing
- Observability for unit economics (cost per document)

Guardrails to state:
- Treat outputs as suggestions; final coding approval remains with staff.
- Ensure traceability: store prompts/outputs per policy; audit access.
- Clarify retention: what must be logged vs what must not be stored.

Suggested success metrics:
- Throughput (docs/hour) and cost per document
- Accuracy proxy metrics defined by customer (e.g., agreement with human coder)

## Claims and language guidance (approved vs. do not say)
### Approved claims (safe)
- “Private deployment options (VPC/on-prem patterns) support strict network boundary requirements.”
- “Audit logging and access controls support regulated operational requirements.”
- “Dedicated capacity provides predictable throughput and isolation for production workloads.”
- “We support security reviews with an FAQ and evidence pack workflow.”

### Use with qualification (ask Security/Legal; do not improvise)
- Any mention of **HIPAA**, **BAA**, “HIPAA-ready,” “HIPAA-compliant,” or “certified.”
- Any claim about **data retention defaults** or specific residency guarantees unless confirmed for the prospect’s deployment mode.
- Any claim implying “we never store anything” or “no logs.”

### Do not say (verbatim)
- “Redwood is HIPAA compliant.”
- “We can sign a BAA for any deployment.”
- “We don’t log anything.”
- “Your data never leaves your environment” (unless Private deployment is confirmed and Security has validated the exact architecture).

## Objection handling
### Objection: “We can’t send PHI to an external API.”
Response:
- “Understood. For strict data boundary requirements, we typically recommend Redwood Private (VPC/on-prem patterns). We can walk through a reference architecture and the controls your security team will expect, including identity, audit logging, encryption, and retention options.”

Next step:
- Schedule security architecture review + confirm network topology requirements.

### Objection: “We need full audit trails and SIEM integration.”
Response:
- “That’s common in regulated environments. We can review our audit logging approach and how customers export logs into their SIEM. We’ll also provide the evidence pack index under NDA so your team can see exactly what artifacts are available.”

### Objection: “We’re worried about model drift and clinical safety.”
Response:
- “We design for controlled rollouts and operational visibility. We’ll align on a prompt/eval set, pin model versions, and use staged rollouts so you can validate quality before expanding.”

### Objection: “We need predictable latency for patient-facing experiences.”
Response:
- “That’s a key reason customers choose Dedicated or Private: predictable throughput and isolation. We can define P95/P99 targets and validate with a short benchmark pilot using your traffic shape.”

## Discovery checklist (healthcare-specific)
Use in first call to qualify for Private/Dedicated and avoid rework.

1) Data classification
- Will PHI be present in prompts/outputs? In logs? In stored datasets?

2) Deployment constraints
- Must run inside customer VPC? On-prem requirements? Private connectivity?

3) Identity and access
- SSO/SAML requirements? SCIM provisioning expectations? Admin separation?

4) Audit + monitoring
- Required audit log fields? SIEM destination? Retention windows?

5) Performance and workload shape
- Interactive vs batch? Peak QPS? Token lengths? Concurrency?

6) Procurement timeline
- Security questionnaire timeline; NDA requirements; contracting sequence
