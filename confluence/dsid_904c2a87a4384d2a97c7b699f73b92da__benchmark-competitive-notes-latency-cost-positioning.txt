Benchmark Competitive Notes: Latency/$ Positioning vs Tokens/sec

## Purpose
This page equips Sales and Solutions Engineering to position the Benchmark Report using **latency/$ (latency per dollar)** as the primary production-economics metric—without getting pulled into misleading “fastest tokens/sec” arguments or making risky comparative claims.

Use this when:
- A prospect challenges the benchmark as “marketing” or “cherry-picked.”
- A competitor claims “we’re faster” based on tokens/sec or a single best-case run.
- A technical evaluator wants to understand why we normalize to dollars and how it maps to their real workload.

> Legal note: Stick to the “Safe language” sections below. Any *named* competitor comparison intended for external sharing must follow the benchmark claims review guidelines.

---

## Core competitive framing
### What we’re measuring (and why it matters)
**Latency/$ answers:** “How much user-perceived latency do I buy for a given spend?”

In production, teams optimize a combined objective:
- **User experience:** p50/p95 latency, tail behavior under load
- **Reliability:** timeouts, throttling, retries, stability across runs/regions
- **Cost:** $/1M tokens (or per request) *at the throughput you actually need*

**Tokens/sec is not enough** because it can:
- Overweight decode-only, long-generation scenarios that aren’t representative for many chat/agent workloads
- Hide queueing and tail latency (a system can be “fast” but spiky)
- Ignore pricing and the overhead required to sustain throughput reliably

### The simplest soundbite
“**Tokens/sec tells you what a GPU can do in a lab. Latency/$ tells you what your app can do in production for a budget.**”

## How to talk about latency/$ vs tokens/sec
### Recommended explanation (30–45 seconds)
“Tokens/sec is useful for understanding raw throughput, but it doesn’t capture what teams ultimately care about: interactive latency at a given cost and reliability level. Latency/$ combines latency and cost so you can compare practical serving efficiency across models and regions. We still show supporting metrics like p50/p95 latency and throughput so you can map it to your workload. The methodology and configs are published so you can reproduce or adapt it.”

### When the prospect insists on tokens/sec
Acknowledge and redirect:
- “Yes—throughput matters. That’s why we include throughput and concurrency in the report.”
- “But tokens/sec alone doesn’t tell you what you’ll pay to meet a p95 SLO.”
- “If your workload is decode-heavy, we can point you to the subset of workloads in the methodology that best match that profile.”

### Practical mapping table
Use this to help evaluators self-select the right lens:

Workload reality | What they’ll ask for | What to emphasize in our report
- Chat/agent UX with SLOs | p95 latency, stability, $ at peak | latency/$ + p95 + variance/stability gates
- Batch summarization | throughput, $/1M tokens | $/1M tokens + throughput under concurrency
- Multi-region product | region consistency, rollouts | regional results + methodology + Dedicated capacity story
- Regulated environment | isolation, predictable spend | Dedicated/Private positioning + reproducibility artifacts

## Typical competitor narratives (and how to respond)
Below are common narratives by competitor category. Keep responses factual, workload-oriented, and anchored to what we publish.

### 1) “We’re faster (tokens/sec) on Model X”
What they mean:
- Best-case throughput with ideal batch size, long decode, warm cache
- Often a single model, single region, single concurrency point

How to respond:
- “We publish p50/p95 and cost-normalized metrics across regions and repeated runs. If you share your target context length, concurrency, and SLO, we can map you to the closest workload profile.”
- “Raw tokens/sec isn’t the same as user-visible latency under load. Tail latency and queueing behavior matter.”

Avoid:
- “We’re always faster.”
- “Their numbers are wrong.”

### 2) “Benchmarks are fake / not real production”
What they mean:
- They don’t trust the prompt set, caching policy, or pricing assumptions

How to respond:
- “That’s why we publish the methodology: prompt sets, warmups, caching/batching policy, and stability checks. You can reproduce the runs or adjust parameters.”
- “The goal isn’t to predict every workload; it’s to provide a credible, repeatable baseline for production economics.”

Offer:
- A lightweight PoC plan: “We’ll replicate with your prompt distribution and SLOs on Hosted, or size Dedicated if you need predictable throughput.”

### 3) “We’re cheaper per token”
What they mean:
- Lower list price, but may omit tail latency, retries, throttling, or required overprovisioning

How to respond:
- “List price is one input. The operational question is: what does it cost to hit your latency and reliability targets at peak?”
- “We show $/1M tokens and latency distributions so you can see the trade-offs.”

### 4) “We have more models / the newest model first”
What they mean:
- Breadth of catalog, novelty advantage

How to respond:
- “Model choice is important; serving economics and reliability are what makes models usable in production. Redwood’s value is predictable, cost-aware inference across hosted, dedicated, and private deployments—with rollouts, fallbacks, and observability.”

### 5) “Self-hosting is cheaper; you’re just a wrapper”
What they mean:
- They compare to theoretical GPU cost without including ops overhead

How to respond:
- “If you can consistently run at high utilization, self-hosting can be great. Many teams underestimate the engineering cost: kernel tuning, batching/caching strategy, autoscaling, multi-region operations, and reliability.”
- “Our benchmark and tooling make the economics transparent. Dedicated and Private give you isolation and predictability without rebuilding the full stack.”

## Safe comparative language (approved patterns)
Use these patterns in emails, calls, and decks.

### Approved
- “In our published methodology, Redwood achieves strong latency/$ across regions for the workloads tested.”
- “For the workloads and configurations described, Redwood shows improved production economics compared to baseline configurations.”
- “Results vary by model, region, context length, and concurrency; we recommend validating against your workload.”
- “We publish the full methodology and artifacts to support reproducibility.”

### Use with extra caution (internal only unless Legal approves)
- “We outperform Provider X on metric Y.” (Requires substantiation and exact conditions; do not send externally without approval.)
- “Best-in-class / industry-leading.” (High-risk, usually needs broad substantiation.)

### Disallowed (do not use)
- “Fastest,” “cheapest,” “#1,” “unbeatable,” “across the board,” “for all workloads.”
- “Competitor is misleading/fraudulent.”
- Any claim that implies a guarantee outside our SLA terms.

## Traps to avoid (and how to steer)
### Trap: “Your latency/$ metric is made up”
Steer to definitions:
- “Latency/$ is a normalization: latency metrics combined with cost metrics under a defined workload. We also show the underlying components (latency distributions, throughput, $/1M tokens).”

### Trap: “You must be using caching/batching tricks”
Steer to production realism:
- “We document caching/batching policy explicitly. Production systems should use them; the question is whether you can do it reliably and predictably.”
- “If your application can’t benefit from caching (e.g., low prompt reuse), we can point you to the relevant slices and discuss expected impact.”

### Trap: “Your benchmark doesn’t match my workload”
Steer to next step:
- “Totally fair—benchmarks are baselines. Let’s map your workload: context length distribution, output length, concurrency, streaming vs non-streaming, and SLO. Then we’ll run a small targeted eval or a Dedicated sizing exercise.”

## Talk tracks by product (Hosted vs Dedicated vs Private)
### Hosted API
Positioning:
- “Fastest path to value + strong economics without operational overhead.”

When it wins:
- PLG trials, fast integration, teams that want performance with minimal ops

Language:
- “You can start with Hosted to validate quality and latency; if you need guaranteed throughput or isolation, we can move to Dedicated.”

### Dedicated
Positioning:
- “Predictable throughput and isolation; consistent performance for production peaks.”

Competitive angle:
- “Dedicated reduces variance and helps you meet p95 SLOs without overprovisioning.”

### Private (VPC/on-prem)
Positioning:
- “Regulated workloads: keep networking and control plane boundaries where you need them, while retaining the same runtime and tooling.”

Competitive angle:
- “Comparable operational tooling and reproducibility without giving up deployment constraints.”

## Quick Q&A (SE-ready)
**Q: Why not just publish tokens/sec?**
A: We include throughput, but tokens/sec alone doesn’t incorporate cost or tail latency. Latency/$ aligns more directly with production decisions.

**Q: Isn’t latency/$ sensitive to pricing assumptions?**
A: Yes, so we document pricing inputs and show component metrics like $/1M tokens. The goal is transparency and repeatability.

**Q: Can customers reproduce the benchmark?**
A: We publish methodology and artifacts, and we can help run a targeted PoC using their prompt sets and SLOs.

## CTA guidance (what to ask for on the call)
To move from “benchmark debate” to “evaluation,” ask for:
- Target regions and data residency constraints
- Context length distribution (p50/p95)
- Output length distribution
- Concurrency / peak RPS expectations
- p95 latency SLO and timeout behavior
- Streaming requirement (yes/no)

Then propose one of:
1) Hosted trial with a workload-matched eval slice
2) Dedicated sizing session (throughput + cost under SLO)
3) Private architecture + security review (if regulated)
