Benchmark Report FAQ + Objection Handling (Latency/$)

## Purpose
This page is the canonical FAQ and objection-handling guide for Sales and Solutions Engineering (SE) when discussing Redwood Inference’s public benchmark report focused on **production inference economics** ("latency/$").

**Use this doc when:**
- Prospects question fairness, workload fit, variance, or reproducibility.
- You need safe language for competitor context.
- You need a clean way to position **Hosted vs Dedicated vs Private** using the benchmark.

**Do not improvise comparative claims.** If a prospect asks for a head-to-head statement that isn’t in the published assets, use the approved language below and offer a proof-based next step (PoC).

---

## Quick positioning (30-second talk track)
“Most teams don’t win on raw tokens/sec; they win on **predictable latency and cost at the quality level they ship**. Our benchmark measures a production-like mix (prefill + decode), across multiple regions, and reports a normalized **latency per dollar** view plus supporting metrics (p50/p95 latency, throughput, and $/1M tokens). We publish methodology and caveats so teams can evaluate fit—and we can help you replicate with your own prompts.”

---

## Definitions (use consistent terms)
**Latency/$ (latency per dollar):** A normalized metric intended to reflect “how much latency you get for a given spend” under a defined workload and run configuration. It is not a universal constant; it changes with context length, concurrency, and decoding behavior.

**Prefill vs decode:**
- *Prefill* = processing input context (often dominates long-context prompts)
- *Decode* = generating output tokens (often dominates long outputs / streaming experiences)

**p50 / p95 latency:** Median vs tail latency. Use p95 when discussing UX guarantees and SLOs.

**Throughput:** Tokens/sec (or requests/sec, depending on view). Important, but incomplete if it ignores tail latency and cost.

**Region:** Geographic deployment location where the run was executed. Network RTT from the end user matters; compute region fit based on your user distribution.

## Approved claims vs. disallowed language
### Approved / safe phrasing
- “Measured under a documented workload and run configuration.”
- “In our published runs…”
- “Results vary by workload characteristics like context length, concurrency, output length, and caching.”
- “We publish caveats and methodology so you can evaluate apples-to-apples.”
- “We can help you reproduce results with your own traffic mix in a PoC.”

### Avoid / do not say
- “We’re the fastest/cheapest, period.”
- “This is a universal ranking.”
- “Competitor X is slower/worse” (unless quoting a published, reviewed, and current claim exactly as written in the report).
- Any implication that results are guaranteed for all workloads or all customer environments.

If pressed for a comparative conclusion beyond what’s published: “We’re careful with broad claims; we can share our methodology and help you run a short benchmark with your prompts and target regions.”

## FAQ: Methodology and fairness
### Q1: What exactly did you measure?
**Answer:** We measured production-relevant inference performance and economics under a defined workload mix. The public report provides:
- Normalized **latency/$**
- Supporting metrics: **p50/p95 latency**, throughput, and **$/1M tokens**
- Breakdowns across **models and regions** included in the published matrix

**Follow-up (SE):** If they ask “what is the single best metric?” say: “There isn’t one. We start with latency/$ because it forces a realistic tradeoff conversation, then validate with p95 and your workload.”

### Q2: Is caching enabled? Are you ‘benchmarking with tricks’?
**Answer:** The benchmark uses a **documented caching and batching policy** consistent with production serving. We explicitly state what is enabled and under what conditions. The intent is not to handicap real-world serving optimizations; it’s to measure what a production operator would actually run.

**If the prospect worries about unfair advantage:**
- “Any optimization we use is part of our production runtime behavior; the goal is to reflect what customers get.”
- “We describe settings and caveats so you can compare to your own configuration.”

### Q3: What concurrency levels did you use?
**Answer:** We test across defined concurrency settings to represent realistic multi-tenant or high-QPS workloads. Concurrency is a first-order driver of batching efficiency and tail latency.

**Key point:** “If your concurrency is materially different (e.g., steady low QPS vs bursty high QPS), we should validate with a short PoC. The directionality of results can change.”

### Q4: Did you warm up the model / exclude cold start?
**Answer:** Yes—runs include warm-up and stability gates to avoid publishing one-off cold-start effects as representative steady-state numbers.

**If cold start matters to them:** “Tell me about your traffic pattern (spiky vs steady). We can test cold-start behavior separately and discuss mitigation (pool sizing, pre-warm, autoscaling policy).”

### Q5: Is this just prefill or just decode? My app is different.
**Answer:** The benchmark is a **mix of prefill and decode** designed to better approximate production distributions. Many real apps swing prefill-heavy (long inputs, retrieval, tools) or decode-heavy (long outputs, creative writing). We recommend validating against your dominant mode:
- Prefill-heavy: long contexts, RAG, tool schemas
- Decode-heavy: long generations, streaming UIs

**Objection handling:** “That’s exactly why we publish supporting metrics and workload notes—so teams can map their use case to the closest slice, then we validate with your prompts.”

### Q6: Do you include streaming?
**Answer:** The public results focus on standardized end-to-end latency metrics and stability. Streaming experiences often care about **time-to-first-token** plus steady token cadence; we treat those as complementary metrics.

**SE note:** If they are a streaming-first product, offer to share a PoC plan that measures TTFT, inter-token latency, and p95 under burst.

### Q7: How do you ensure runs are stable and not cherry-picked?
**Answer:** We use repeat runs, variance thresholds, and rerun gates. If tail latency varies beyond a threshold, the run is automatically re-executed or flagged. We also track run manifests and validation status.

**Sound bite:** “We’d rather publish fewer numbers we can defend than more numbers we can’t explain.”

### Q8: Why should I trust a vendor’s benchmark?
**Answer:** Healthy skepticism is warranted. We try to earn trust by:
- Publishing methodology and caveats
- Using repeatable run manifests
- Providing supporting metrics beyond a single score
- Offering reproducibility guidance (see below)

**Next step:** “If you share your target model(s), region(s), and traffic mix, we’ll run a joint PoC and review results together.”

## FAQ: Reproducibility and “can I replicate this?”
### Q9: Can customers reproduce the numbers?
**Answer:** You can reproduce *directional* performance with the same workload assumptions; exact matching is not guaranteed due to variance drivers (region capacity, hardware pools, kernel choices, background load).

**Recommended replication approach (SE-ready):**
1) Pick your top 1–2 models and 1–2 regions.
2) Use your own representative prompt set (or map to ours if you prefer a baseline).
3) Run a 60–120 minute window with burst + steady segments.
4) Measure p50/p95, TTFT (if streaming), and $/1M tokens.
5) Compare relative results and confirm SLO fit.

### Q10: What artifacts are retained for substantiation?
**Answer:** Redwood retains run manifests, configs, and derived datasets used for public claims under an internal retention policy with access controls.

**If asked for raw logs:** “We generally don’t share raw internal logs, but we can share methodology, manifests/config excerpts as appropriate, and run a customer-specific PoC with transparent settings.”

## FAQ: Variance, regions, and “why does this number differ from what I see?”
### Q11: Why is p95 sometimes noisy across runs?
**Answer:** Tail latency is sensitive to queueing, batching dynamics, kernel selection, and transient infrastructure effects. We mitigate noise with repeat runs and stability gates, but some variance remains.

**How to explain without over-technical detail:**
- “p95 captures worst-case user experiences; it moves more than the median.”
- “We use repeat runs and reruns to avoid publishing outliers.”

### Q12: Why are results different across regions?
**Answer:** Region differences come from (a) network distance to users, (b) hardware fleet characteristics, and (c) capacity conditions. The right region is the one that meets your user latency SLO and residency requirements.

**If they want ‘one best region’:** “There isn’t one—choose based on where your users and compliance needs are. The report helps you make that tradeoff explicit.”

### Q13: Our internal testing shows different cost or latency. Is the benchmark wrong?
**Answer:** Not necessarily. Common reasons:
- Different context length distribution
- Different output lengths (decode-heavy vs prefill-heavy)
- Different concurrency / burst pattern
- Different region, model version, or quantization profile
- Different caching characteristics (prompt reuse)

**SE next step:** “Let’s compare your test setup to the benchmark assumptions and adjust. We can usually explain the delta quickly.”

## FAQ: Competitors and comparative positioning (safe language)
### Q14: Does this benchmark compare against [Competitor]?
**Answer:** The public report may include contextual baselines where appropriate, with explicit caveats. We avoid overgeneralized claims because configs and workloads differ significantly across platforms.

**If the prospect pushes for a “who wins?” answer:**
- “The honest answer depends on your workload mix and regions.”
- “We can run a short, apples-to-apples PoC using your prompts to settle it.”

### Q15: Competitor says tokens/sec is the only thing that matters.
**Answer:** Tokens/sec is useful, but incomplete:
- It can improve by increasing batching at the expense of p95 latency.
- It doesn’t include economics ($/token) or a normalized view.
- It can hide prefill vs decode tradeoffs.

**Suggested response:** “We care about throughput too, but we optimize for the metric your end users feel and your finance team pays for: p95 latency at a given cost.”

### Q16: “Latency/$ sounds like marketing math.”
**Answer:** It’s a convenience metric, not a magic number. We include the underlying metrics (p50/p95, throughput, $/1M tokens) so you can validate the parts independently.

**Line to use:** “Treat latency/$ as the index that points you to the best options, then confirm with the raw metrics for your SLO.”

## Objection handling by deployment: Hosted vs Dedicated vs Private
### Q17: Are these benchmark numbers from Hosted or Dedicated?
**Answer:** The report reflects Redwood’s production serving stack under the stated configuration and environment. When prospects require stronger guarantees (isolation, predictable throughput, residency), we recommend evaluating Dedicated or Private.

**Decision guidance:**
- **Hosted API:** Fastest time-to-value; best for prototypes → production when shared tenancy is acceptable.
- **Dedicated:** Best when you need predictable throughput, isolation, and SLA-backed performance for steady workloads.
- **Private (VPC/on-prem):** Best for regulated workloads, strict network controls, or data residency constraints.

### Q18: “We need strict isolation—does the benchmark apply?”
**Answer:** Use the benchmark as directional evidence of runtime efficiency, then position **Dedicated** for isolation and predictable capacity.

**Talk track:** “The core serving runtime is the same; Dedicated adds reserved capacity and isolation so you’re not sharing fleet dynamics with other tenants.”

### Q19: “We can’t send data to a hosted API.”
**Answer:** Position **Private**:
- “We support private deployments (VPC and on-prem options) with customer-managed networking and audit controls.”
- “Benchmark results show what the runtime can do; Private performance depends on your hardware profile and topology, and we’ll size it with you.”

### Q20: “Your benchmark is great, but can you meet our SLO in region X?”
**Answer:** Commit to a validation plan, not a promise:
- “Let’s confirm region availability and then run a targeted load test with your request shape.”
- “If you need hard guarantees, Dedicated capacity in that region is the path.”

## Common prospect questions (short answers)
### Q21: Which models are included?
**Answer:** A curated list of commonly deployed open models and variants. The exact set is defined in the report and interactive page; we avoid publishing results for configurations we can’t operationally support.

### Q22: How often will you update the benchmark?
**Answer:** Periodically. Model releases and runtime improvements can change results; we will avoid frequent “churn” updates that reduce interpretability. If a material change occurs, we publish an update/addendum.

### Q23: Are results “guaranteed” in production?
**Answer:** No. Results are measurements under defined conditions. We can propose an architecture and capacity plan to meet your SLO, and for Dedicated we can contract SLAs.

## SE appendix: Discovery questions to quickly map workload fit
Ask these before debating any metric:
1) Primary user regions (top 2) and data residency requirements
2) Typical input tokens (p50/p95) and output tokens (p50/p95)
3) Concurrency pattern: steady QPS, burst, or spiky
4) Streaming UX requirements (TTFT, p95)
5) Reliability needs: fallback allowed? multi-region?
6) Deployment constraints: Hosted vs Dedicated vs Private
