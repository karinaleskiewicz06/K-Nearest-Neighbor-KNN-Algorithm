Postmortem: Streaming responses stalling on long contexts (continuous batching regression)

h1. Summary
On 2026-02-07 a subset of streaming chat completions stalled (no tokens emitted for >30s) for requests with long input contexts. The incident was triggered by a serving-runtime change to continuous batching fairness that interacted poorly with high-concurrency workloads.

*Customer impact:* Yes (timeouts and degraded UX for streaming)
*Severity:* SEV-1
*Owning engineering team:* Serving Runtime
*Primary components:* runtime-scheduler, stream-forwarder, kv-cache-manager

h1. Impact
* ~4.5% of streaming requests in us-east stalled; clients timed out after 60s
* Elevated support tickets from 6 customers (Enterprise + PLG)
* No data integrity issues

h1. Timeline (UTC)
* 18:04 - Alert: streaming token rate anomaly
* 18:07 - On-call confirms stalled streams in traces
* 18:12 - Identified correlation with long-context prompts and high queue depth
* 18:18 - Rolled back runtime-scheduler build rwr-1.12.3 to rwr-1.12.2
* 18:29 - Streaming stabilized; backlog drained
* 18:45 - Incident resolved

h1. Root cause
A fairness tweak to continuous batching prioritized newly-arrived short requests too aggressively. Under high concurrency, long-context requests repeatedly yielded before first token emission, effectively starving the stream-forwarder. This increased time-to-first-token beyond client timeouts for the affected requests.

h1. Detection
Detected by automated metric: stream_forwarder_tokens_per_second drop combined with p99 time_to_first_token increase.

h1. Corrective actions
1) Add starvation protection for long-context streams in runtime-scheduler (Owner: Serving Runtime; Due: 2026-02-20)
2) Add canary workload that includes long-context + high concurrency to perf-canary (Owner: Applied ML; Due: 2026-03-01)
3) Update release checklist to require time_to_first_token regression gate (Owner: Release Eng; Due: 2026-02-22)

h1. Notes
This postmortem is scoped to customer-impacting streaming stalls. We excluded internal-only benchmark anomalies.
