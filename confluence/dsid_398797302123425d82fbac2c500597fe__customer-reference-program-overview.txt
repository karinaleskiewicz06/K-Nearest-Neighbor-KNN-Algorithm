Customer Reference Program (Design Partners) — Overview & Charter

## Purpose
Redwood Inference is launching a structured **Customer Reference + Case Study Design Partner Program** to accelerate enterprise pipeline and improve conversion for **Dedicated** and **Private** deployments.

This page is the program charter (why we’re doing this, who qualifies, what we ask from customers, and how we run it). For the operational workflow (intake, approvals, scheduling, tracking), see **Customer Reference Intake & Approval Process**.

---

## Program goals (what success looks like)
**Primary goals (8–10 week push):**
- Secure **3–5 signed reference agreements** (approved for reference calls and/or public assets).
- Publish **2–3 public case studies** (logo + approved quotes; metrics if allowed).
- Enable Sales/CS to consistently request and use references without customer fatigue.

**Business outcomes we are targeting:**
- Increase late-stage conversion for Dedicated/Private opportunities.
- Shorten security/procurement cycles by providing credible peer validation.
- Improve “why Redwood” clarity with proof points on **latency/cost/reliability/deployment flexibility**.

**Program KPIs (tracked weekly):**
- # signed reference agreements (target: 3–5)
- # published public case studies (target: 2–3)
- Reference call utilization (requests/month; target to set after baseline)
- Deal influence (tagged opportunities where reference assets/calls were used)
- Qualitative feedback from AEs/CSMs (usefulness + friction points)

## Target audiences and personas
**Primary buyer personas we want to influence:**
- VP Engineering / Head of Platform

## What “Design Partner” means at Redwood
A **Design Partner** in this program is a customer who:
1) Has achieved a meaningful production or near-production outcome with Redwood (Hosted/Dedicated/Private), and
2) Agrees to participate in one or more **reference activities** with clear boundaries, and
3) Provides explicit permissions (legal-approved) for the allowed uses: logo, quotes, metrics ranges, etc.

**Important:** This is not a product beta program by default. A design partner may provide roadmap feedback, but the program’s core purpose is **go-to-market proof** (reliable references and publishable stories).

---

## Reference activities (how references will be used)
Design Partners can opt into one or more of the following. Permission is explicitly captured in the consent language.

### 1) Reference calls (most common)
Used by AEs/CSMs for late-stage opportunities.
- Format: 30 minutes (occasionally 45 minutes)
- Typical attendees: prospect technical lead(s) + Redwood AE + Redwood SE (optional)
- Goal: validate operational reality (SLA posture, reliability, cost/latency tradeoffs, deployment constraints)

**Guardrails:**
- No “surprise” scheduling. Customer must have adequate context and an approved brief.
- Avoid asking customers to disclose confidential terms, pricing, or sensitive architecture.

### 2) Public case study (docs site + PDF)
Used for inbound validation, outbound sequences, and late-stage decks.
- Includes: problem, constraints, solution, implementation, results (or outcomes), quotes, and an architecture diagram if approved.
- Metrics: can be exact numbers **only if approved**; otherwise use ranges/relative improvements or qualitative outcomes.

### 3) Quotes / testimonials (web + decks)
Short approved statements for:
- Website (case study pages, landing pages)
- Sales decks (late-stage validation)
- Product announcements (when relevant)

### 4) Events (optional, higher lift)
Possible formats:
- Joint webinar
- Conference talk / panel
- Partner co-marketing (requires separate approvals)

## Story angles & proof points (what we want to capture)
We prioritize stories that map to Redwood’s approved messaging pillars:

1) **Performance economics**
- Lower $/1M tokens or lower cost per request
- Higher throughput at similar latency
- Better batching/caching efficiency with stable user experience

2) **Reliability and graceful degradation**
- Fallback routing policies
- Multi-region considerations
- Incident posture and mitigations that avoid customer-visible impact

3) **Deployment flexibility (Hosted / Dedicated / Private)**
- Why the customer chose Dedicated (predictable capacity, data isolation)
- Why the customer chose Private/VPC (network controls, auditability, compliance)

4) **Operational tooling (Console + Optimize)**
- Token-level latency/cost breakdown
- Dashboards, tracing hooks, and rollouts
- Regression monitoring and evaluation harness usage

**Proof requirements (internal):**
- Claims must be backed by: console screenshots, benchmark/eval results, ticket references, or a documented measurement method.
- Any security/compliance statements must be reviewed by Security/Legal and tied to what is actually enabled in the customer deployment.

## Eligibility criteria (who is a good candidate)
A candidate should meet most of the criteria below.

### Required
- **Relationship health:** CSM confirms account is in good standing (no active escalation that would make an ask inappropriate).
- **Meaningful usage:** production, pilot, or committed rollout with a real workload (not a purely speculative POC).
- **Clear story angle:** at least one compelling pillar (cost, latency, reliability, deployment/security, operations).
- **Champion identified:** someone willing to speak and help navigate internal approvals.

### Strongly preferred
- Evidence of differentiated outcome vs alternatives (e.g., measurable latency reduction, improved throughput, simplified compliance posture).
- Executive sponsor on customer side can approve logo/quote usage.
- Willingness to share metrics as exact values or at least ranges.

### Disqualifiers / pause conditions
- Active Sev-1/Sev-2 escalation or unresolved trust issue.
- No clear champion or internal buyer alignment.
- Legal/compliance categorically refuses any public attribution (may still be eligible for **private reference calls only**, subject to constraints).

## Customer ask (what we request from Design Partners)
We aim to make participation lightweight and predictable.

**Typical package for a public case study + reference calls:**
- 1× 30–45 min story interview (Marketing + Solutions Eng optional)
- 1× 30 min technical validation (architecture review + claims check)
- Up to 2 review rounds on draft write-up (with a defined review window)
- Optional: 1–2 reference calls/month (with a cooldown policy)

**Customer experience principles:**
- Clear agenda and prep materials for every interaction
- Minimal back-and-forth: we propose language that is easy to approve
- Respect confidentiality: no sensitive metrics or security details without explicit permission

## Incentives (what Redwood offers)
Incentives are optional and should be used thoughtfully (not as a quid-pro-quo for specific claims). Any incentive must be approved per Finance/Legal guidance.

**Approved incentive options (choose one, when appropriate):**
- **Conference sponsorship credit** (e.g., booth add-on or speaking support; scope capped by budget)
- **Extended Redwood Optimize trial** (or extended access to specific Optimize capabilities, where commercially feasible)
- **Charitable donation** in the customer’s name (subject to policy and compliance)

**Notes:**
- Incentive type and amount must be recorded in the program tracker.
- Government/public-sector entities may have restrictions; consult Legal before offering anything.

## Permissions, approvals, and boundaries
**We do not use any customer logo, name, quote, or identifiable metrics publicly without explicit written consent.**

### Permissions we capture (by customer)
- Logo usage (where + how)
- Quote usage (approved text + attribution)
- Case study publication approval (web + PDF)
- Metrics disclosure (exact, ranges, relative improvements, or “no metrics”)
- Reference calls (allowed topics + avoid list)

### Typical review window
- Draft review: **5–10 business days** (may be longer for regulated customers)
- Final approval: **confirmed in writing** (email is acceptable if Legal agrees; preferred is the consent form)

### Customer load management
To avoid overburdening reference customers:
- Default limit: **≤ 2 reference calls per month per customer**
- **Cooldown** after a heavy period or if customer signals fatigue
- Opt-out at any time; CSM owns relationship-based escalation

## Timeline (8–10 week launch plan)
**Week 1–2: Program design + shortlist**
- Finalize eligibility criteria, incentives, and guardrails
- Confirm templates: consent form, reference-call brief, case study outline
- Build candidate shortlist from Sales/CS/Partnerships

- Draft case study narrative + diagram + metrics worksheet
- Internal proof/claims review against messaging pillars

**Week 7–9: Approvals + publication**
- Customer review cycles and redlines
- Legal sign-off on final permissions
- Publish to docs site + generate 1-pager PDF

**Week 9–10: Enablement + operationalize intake**
- Sales/CS training: how to request, prep, and log reference usage
- Launch ongoing intake workflow + reporting dashboard

## Risks & mitigations
- **Confidentiality / compliance constraints** → Offer private reference calls only; use anonymized or range-based metrics; pre-review sensitive language.
- **Uneven willingness to share metrics** → Prioritize strong qualitative outcomes and operational proof (SLO adherence, reduced incidents, simplified deployment).
- **Time zones / scheduling delays** → Use defined review windows; pre-book interview slots; keep drafts tight.
- **Customer fatigue** → Enforce call caps and cooldowns; ensure every call request includes deal context and a clear ask.

## Appendix: Example approved boundaries (talking points)
Use these as defaults unless the customer’s consent specifies otherwise.

**Okay to discuss (typical):**
- Why they chose Dedicated/Private vs alternatives (at a high level)
- Operational experience: latency consistency, reliability posture, fallbacks
- Integration experience: API/SDK, rollout process, observability

**Avoid unless explicitly approved:**
- Exact pricing, contract terms, or vendor comparisons beyond general reasoning
- Sensitive workload details (PII/PHI), internal security controls, pen test results
- Exact infrastructure topology or capacity numbers that could be identifying
