Regulated Inference Competitive Battlecard (Dedicated + Private)

## Purpose
This battlecard is for **security- and compliance-driven buyers** evaluating LLM inference in regulated environments. Use it to:
- Position **Redwood Dedicated** and **Redwood Private (VPC/on-prem)** vs common alternatives
- Avoid common competitive traps in “private / compliant / enterprise-ready” claims
- Run a discovery motion that quickly qualifies for Dedicated vs Private and accelerates security review

## TL;DR positioning (what we are / are not)
**Redwood Inference** is a developer-first inference platform optimized for production economics and reliability, with consistent operations across:
- **Hosted API** (fast start)
- **Dedicated capacity** (predictable throughput + isolation)
- **Private deployments** (VPC and on-prem options for regulated workloads)

**We win in regulated deals when** the prospect needs: deployment flexibility + operational tooling (auditability, controls, rollouts) without building and operating an entire serving stack.

**We do not win when** the prospect only wants “any model endpoint” with minimal requirements, or when compliance constraints require features we don’t support in the current contract/SOW.

## Audience + buying committee (who you’ll meet)
Typical personas and what they care about:
- **CISO / Security / GRC:** auditability, key management, access controls (SSO/SAML), incident response, vendor evidence, data handling, retention/residency
- **Platform / ML Engineering:** latency/throughput SLOs, predictable capacity, runtime performance, observability, routing/fallback, rollout safety
- **Procurement / Legal:** DPA/SLA, liability caps, subprocessors, residency terms, support tiers, price predictability

## Redwood differentiators for regulated deployments (approved themes)
Use these themes consistently; do not over-claim.

### 1) Deployment flexibility without changing the developer workflow
- Same product surface area and operational model across Hosted/Dedicated/Private.
- **Dedicated** for predictable throughput + data isolation on reserved GPU pools.
- **Private** for customer-controlled networking boundaries (VPC) and on-prem options when required.

### 2) Operational tooling that security teams can validate
- **Audit logs** and clear event semantics (who/what/when) with export patterns.
- **Role-based access control** and enterprise identity patterns (SSO/SAML; SCIM where applicable per current docs).
- **KMS/HSM integration patterns** for key management (confirm exact mode in Security FAQ / evidence pack).

### 3) Production economics you can measure (not just benchmark slides)
- Token-level visibility into latency and cost drivers.
- Workload-aware batching/caching and routing policies.
- Rollouts (canary, A/B, fallback policies) to reduce outage risk and regression risk.

### 4) Reliability + graceful degradation
- Policy-based routing and fallback to keep apps online during capacity constraints.
- Clear SLO targets for Dedicated and implementation/runbook expectations for Private.

## Competitive landscape for regulated inference
We generally encounter four competitor “buckets.” The right response depends on the buyer’s constraint (security boundary, procurement path, performance SLOs).

### A) Cloud hyperscaler managed AI platforms (e.g., AWS/Azure/GCP managed endpoints)
**Their positioning:** “Integrated with your cloud + enterprise controls + easy procurement.”

**Where they’re strong:**
- Existing cloud security posture and procurement rails
- Broad services ecosystem
- Easy to get started for non-strict workloads

**Typical gaps in regulated inference deals (discovery-led):**
- Operational depth for LLM serving is often fragmented across services (observability, rollouts, fallback patterns are DIY).
- Capacity predictability and latency consistency can be hard at scale without significant platform work.
- “Private” language may mean different things (shared control plane, multi-tenant assumptions, ambiguous audit semantics).

**Redwood counter (talk track):**
- “If you’re already all-in on the hyperscaler, we can still be the **inference runtime + operations layer** that makes performance and reliability predictable—especially when you need Dedicated or Private boundaries with consistent tooling.”

**Trap to avoid:** Don’t claim the hyperscaler is “non-compliant.” Instead: “The question isn’t whether it can be compliant; it’s whether you can achieve your **specific controls + operational requirements** with minimal engineering lift and timeline risk.”

### B) LLM API providers (public APIs)
**Their positioning:** “Best models + fastest integration.”

**Where they’re strong:**
- Fast onboarding, strong model quality perception
- Minimal infra ownership

**Typical gaps in regulated deals:**
- Limited deployment modes (may not meet VPC/on-prem constraints)
- Auditability, retention/residency controls, and evidence workflows may not match strict requirements
- Harder to align to customer’s internal boundary definitions

**Redwood counter:**
- “When security needs a clear boundary (VPC/on-prem), and engineering needs predictable throughput, Redwood Dedicated/Private provides the same developer experience with the controls and evidence path regulated teams expect.”

**Trap to avoid:** Do not promise “no data is ever stored” unless confirmed by the Security FAQ and the specific contract configuration.

### C) Inference platform vendors (managed inference, ‘enterprise endpoints’)
**Their positioning:** “We host open models with strong performance; enterprise-ready deployments available.”

**Where they’re strong:**
- Good performance marketing
- Model catalog breadth

**Common issues we see:**
- “VPC” can mean many things: shared control plane, limited customer-managed networking, unclear blast radius.
- Lack of security-review-ready artifacts (or slow turnaround on questionnaires).
- Operational tooling gaps: rollouts, fallback, audit semantics, retention controls.

**Redwood counter:**
- “We’re built around production operations: rollouts, fallback, token-level cost/latency, and a security evidence process tailored for regulated buyers. You’ll spend less time reverse-engineering what you’re getting.”

### D) Build-it-yourself / open-source runtime (vLLM, TGI, custom serving)
**Their positioning:** “Full control, lowest unit cost (in theory), no vendor risk.”

**Where they’re strong:**
- Maximum configurability
- Fits teams with deep infra maturity and long time horizons

**The reality (qualify quickly):**
- Regulated requirements add a lot beyond “serve tokens”: audit logging, identity, key management, retention, evidence, change management, SLO monitoring, on-call, vulnerability patching.
- Hidden costs: reliability engineering, capacity planning, rollouts, perf tuning, multi-region, incident response.

**Redwood counter:**
- “If you want full control without a multi-quarter platform build, Redwood gives you the controls and operational maturity out of the box—while keeping deployment optionality (Dedicated now, Private when security requires it).”

**Trap to avoid:** Don’t dismiss self-hosting. Instead: “Happy to compare total cost of ownership and timeline risk; we can run a scoped benchmark + architecture validation in 2–3 weeks.”

## Common competitive traps in regulated deals (and how to neutralize)
1) **‘We are HIPAA-ready / compliant’ language**
   - Use: “We support common controls for regulated workloads; specifics depend on your configuration and legal terms.”
   - Avoid: “HIPAA certified,” “guaranteed compliant,” “fully compliant out of the box.”

2) **‘Private VPC’ ambiguity**
   - Ask: “Is the control plane shared? Who has access? What logs are produced and where do they export? What is the isolation model?”

3) **Audit logs that are not security-grade**
   - Ask for: field-level schema, export mechanism, retention behavior, and how identity is represented.

4) **KMS claims without clear key boundary**
   - Ask: “Is it customer-managed keys? Envelope encryption? BYOK? Is rotation supported? What’s the operational model?”

5) **‘Dedicated’ that is not actually predictable**
   - Ask: “Is capacity reserved? What are throughput commitments? What happens during burst? What are the SLOs and measurement windows?”

6) **Security questionnaire turnaround as a deal-killer**
   - Offer: a pre-scoped evidence pack index + a live security review with Security/GRC on the first call.

## Redwood win themes by buyer type
### CISO / Security / GRC
- Clear controls: audit logs, access controls, retention/residency options, key management integration patterns
- Fast evidence workflow (what we can share, how we share it, redaction policy)
- Reduced operational risk: fallback, controlled rollouts, incident response readiness

### Platform / ML Engineering
- Predictable throughput (Dedicated) and defined boundaries (Private)
- Runtime optimizations + observability to tune cost/latency
- Less time building ops: dashboards, tracing hooks, error analysis, rollout guardrails

### Procurement / Legal

- Standard security artifacts + DPA/SLA workflow

## Recommended discovery questions (use these to qualify fast)
Use these early; they map directly to Dedicated vs Private and reduce back-and-forth.

### Security boundary and data handling
1) “What is the strictest boundary requirement: public API, VPC-only, or on-prem/air-gapped?”
2) “Do you consider prompts/outputs/embeddings to be regulated data (e.g., PHI/PII/PCI)?”
3) “What are your **data retention** expectations for inference logs, prompts, and outputs?”
4) “Any explicit **data residency** or regional isolation requirements?”
5) “Do you require **customer-managed keys** (KMS/HSM) and key rotation policies?”

### Auditability and access controls
6) “What events must be logged for audit: user access, config changes, inference requests, admin actions?”
7) “What’s your identity standard: SSO/SAML? SCIM provisioning? RBAC granularity needs?”
8) “Do you need audit log export to SIEM (and in what format)?”

### Reliability and performance
9) “What are your latency targets (p50/p95) and throughput needs (RPS / tokens per second)?”
10) “Is usage spiky (interactive) or batch-heavy? Any hard peak windows?”
11) “What’s the failure tolerance—can you degrade to a smaller model/region, or must responses always be ‘full fidelity’?”

### Procurement and timeline
12) “What’s the target go-live date, and what is the security review critical path?”
13) “Do you need an SLA? What are your uptime/response-time expectations?”
14) “Preferred commercial route: direct, marketplace/private offer, or existing MSA?”

## Qualification: Dedicated vs Private (quick guidance)
Recommend **Dedicated** when:
- They want **predictable throughput** and isolation but can operate within Redwood-managed environment.
- They have strict SLOs and want a fast path to production.

Recommend **Private (VPC / on-prem)** when:
- They require **customer-controlled networking boundaries** and/or on-prem constraints.
- They have strict residency/retention rules that require private control plane alignment.

If unclear, propose: “Start Dedicated for performance validation + operational workflow, then move to Private once security approves the boundary.”

## Competitive objection handling (copy/paste)
**Objection: “We can just use our cloud’s managed AI service.”**
- “Totally viable for many teams. The question is whether you can meet your latency/throughput targets and security controls *without* building a large amount of serving operations yourself. Redwood is the inference runtime + operations layer that makes those outcomes predictable across Dedicated and Private boundaries.”

**Objection: “We’ll self-host vLLM for full control.”**
- “If you have the platform bandwidth, that can work. In regulated environments the heavy lift is usually auditability, identity, retention, evidence, and safe rollouts on top of the serving runtime. We can run a scoped benchmark + architecture validation so you can compare timeline and TCO with real numbers.”

**Objection: “Your competitor says they’re ‘VPC private’ too.”**
- “Happy to compare isolation models. Can we align on what ‘private’ means for your security team—control plane, audit log semantics, key boundaries, and export paths? We’ll walk through our reference architecture and the evidence pack index.”

## Do / Do not say (compliance-safe language)
**Do say:**
- “We support common controls for regulated workloads; details depend on configuration and contractual terms.”
- “We can provide an evidence pack under appropriate confidentiality.”
- “We offer Dedicated capacity and Private deployment options (VPC/on-prem).”

**Do not say:**
- “HIPAA certified / guaranteed compliant”
- “Zero retention” or “we never store data” unless explicitly validated for the customer’s configuration and documented in the current Security FAQ
- “Air-gapped on-prem is turnkey” (only commit based on current Private deployment packaging and SE validation)
