Incident Review: Fallback routing to legacy quantized variant during traffic surge (BrightHealthAI, 2026-03-12)

h1. Overview
On 2026-03-12, a customer traffic surge for BrightHealthAI (dedicated tier) caused elevated p95/p99 latency and request timeouts. A subset of requests were routed away from the pinned primary model variant to a legacy quantized compatible variant (redwood/quant-13b-q4). The quantized variant incurred repeated cold starts and had lower throughput, amplifying tail latency under load.

This document summarizes impact, timeline, contributing factors, root cause, and the remediation shipped after the event.

h2. Customer / Environment
* Customer: BrightHealthAI (dedicated)
* Environment: prod-us-east primary (spillover observed in us-west)
* Components: serving-runtime, smart-routing, api-gateway, scheduler/autoscaler
* Primary pinned model: redwood/rlhf-vicuna-13b (deployed train v2026-02-08; serving config hash updated 2026-03-05)
* Fallback variant observed: redwood/quant-13b-q4 (legacy quantized variant; no pre-warm pool at the time)

h2. Impact Summary (updated)
* Duration: 2026-03-12T18:33Z to ~19:00Z (most severe); residual elevated p99 until ~19:07Z due to client retries and draining queues.
* Error rate: ~7% of requests returned 504s at peak (previously estimated 10–20% during initial triage).
* Latency: p95 increased from ~0.9s baseline to ~3.8s; p99 peaked >10s for a subset of routed-to-quant traffic.
* Scope: BrightHealthAI dedicated pool traffic in us-east; limited spillover in us-west due to global routing retries.
* Secondary impact: increased billing risk due to client retry behavior (2 retries with short backoff), and increased load on router/serving queues.

h2. Detection
* Customer-reported: BrightHealthAI reported 504s starting ~18:45Z, noting a scheduled batch unexpectedly spiked traffic.
* Internal signals: router logs showed fallback routing reasons shifting to "capacity_unavailable" and then "compatible_variant"; serving-runtime showed cold-start spikes and queue growth (>250 pending on some workers).

h2. Timeline (UTC)
* 18:32: Traffic spike begins (customer scheduled batch misconfiguration).
* 18:33–18:35: Smart-routing begins returning x-redwood-routing-reason: capacity_unavailable for the pinned pool; some requests routed to compatible legacy quant variant.
* 18:45: Customer reports 504s and asks whether requests were routed away from pinned model.
* 18:52: Support opens P1 and begins collecting request IDs/trace samples.
* 19:01: SRE confirms fallback routing to redwood/quant-13b-q4 and cold-start/queue pressure in serving-runtime.
* 19:02: Temporary mitigation applied: per-customer routing override to enforce strict pinning (no fallback to legacy quant).
* 19:07: Customer confirms immediate improvement; errors drop near-zero.
* 19:20: Customer acknowledges; agrees to update retry/backoff behavior.
* 2026-03-13: Engineering creates ENG-3124 (routing logic) and ENG-3140 (telemetry).
* 2026-03-18: ENG-3124 merged and rolled out to prod-us-east, then prod-us-west.
* 2026-03-20: ENG-3140 merged and rolled out; new headers/metrics available.

h2. Technical Details
h3. What happened (routing + serving)
During the surge, the dedicated pool for the pinned model temporarily exhausted warm capacity. Smart-routing interpreted this as "capacity_unavailable" and selected a compatible alternative variant (legacy quant). The legacy quantized variant:
* Lacked a warm pool at the time.
* Had higher cold-start time (observed ~3.2s average cold start during the incident window).
* Had lower effective throughput, causing queue growth and timeouts.

Client retry behavior (short 200–500ms backoff) amplified the pressure and increased tail latency.

h3. Observations / Signals
* Response headers observed by the customer: x-redwood-variant and x-redwood-routing-reason.
* Router logs: fallback routing reason "capacity_unavailable" then "compatible_variant" between ~18:33Z and ~19:00Z.
* Serving runtime: repeated cold starts for quant workers; queue depth spikes >250; workers timing out under load.

h2. Root Cause (final)
Smart-routing permitted fallback from a strictly pinned dedicated deployment to a "compatible" variant when the pinned pool reported transient capacity exhaustion. The selection algorithm did not sufficiently weight (or explicitly gate on) warm-pool capability and cold-start risk for candidate variants. Under high QPS, routing to a non-warmable legacy variant converted a short-lived capacity dip into sustained tail-latency and timeouts.

h2. Contributing Factors
* Autoscaler warmup policy did not pre-load or pre-warm the legacy quant variant; newly created workers entered service without mitigating cold-start penalties.
* Variant metadata did not explicitly expose warm-pool capability to routing decisions (prior to ENG-3140).
* Client retries used short backoff, effectively multiplying load during the period of degraded service.
* Dedicated pool had insufficient surge headroom for the customer’s unexpected batch.

h2. Mitigation (during incident)
* Applied per-customer routing override to enforce strict pinning for BrightHealthAI (blocked fallback to redwood/quant-13b-q4).
* Recommended customer retry change: exponential backoff with jitter and longer initial delay.

h2. Remediation (post-incident)
h3. Shipped changes
* ENG-3124 (Routing guardrails):
  * Added routing constraints so that for dedicated + pinned configurations, fallback to legacy quant variants is disabled unless explicitly allowed by policy.
  * Updated candidate scoring to prefer warm-pool-capable variants under capacity pressure; non-warmable variants are deprioritized and can be excluded when latency SLO risk is high.
* ENG-3140 (Telemetry):
  * Added router telemetry fields/metrics to record (a) warm-pool capability, (b) variant selection score breakdown, and (c) whether a fallback was blocked by policy.
  * Exposed additional debugging headers for internal use (restricted to trusted callers): x-redwood-warm-pool-capable and x-redwood-variant-score.

h3. Operational changes
* Updated fallback routing runbook to include a "Dedicated + pinned" decision tree and explicit instructions for applying per-customer strict pinning overrides.
* Added an on-call checklist item to capture: routed variant, routing reason, warm-pool capability, and queue depth.

h2. Customer Communication
