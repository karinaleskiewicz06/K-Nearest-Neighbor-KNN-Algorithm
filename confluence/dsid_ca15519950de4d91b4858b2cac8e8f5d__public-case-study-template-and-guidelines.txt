Public Case Study Template & Writing Guidelines

## Purpose
This page is the standard template and set of guidelines for writing **public** Redwood Inference customer case studies. It is optimized for a developer-first audience while meeting our proof, legal, and brand requirements.

Use this for:
- Website/docs “Customer Stories” pages
- PDF 1-pagers for Sales
- Blog posts that mirror the customer story structure

Not in scope:
- Internal-only deal notes (use HubSpot)
- Long-form solution briefs (use Product Marketing templates)

---

## Audience and success criteria
**Primary reader:** Engineering and ML leaders evaluating Redwood (VP Eng, Head of ML, Staff Eng, platform owners).

A good case study should:
1) State the **problem** and why it mattered (latency, cost, reliability, compliance, deployment constraints).
2) Explain the **implementation** in a way that a senior engineer finds credible.
3) Provide **proof** (metrics, measured outcomes, ranges, or clearly labeled qualitative results).
4) Make it easy for Sales/CS to reuse (clear claims, quotes, diagram, approved disclaimer language).

---

## Output formats (pick one)
**A) Web case study (default):** 800–1,400 words, skimmable headings, 1 architecture diagram, 2–3 quotes.

**B) PDF 1-pager (sales handout):** 350–600 words + diagram + results callout.

**C) “Public but anonymized” story:** Same structure, but no logo/company name. Use only if Legal/CS confirms this is acceptable and the customer agrees to the anonymization approach.

---

## Required artifacts checklist (must have before publish)
**Required**
- Customer-approved **logo usage** (or approved anonymization)
- 1–3 approved **quotes** with attribution rules (name/title/company or anonymized)
- **Architecture diagram** (see requirements below)
- **Results section** with at least one of:
  - publishable metric(s)
  - publishable metric ranges
  - qualitative outcomes with “how measured” notes
- **Proof pack** (internal): links to dashboards, evals, run logs, tickets, or measurement methodology
- **Approvals**: AE + CSM + Product Marketing + Solutions Eng + Legal (logo/quotes/attribution)

**Strongly recommended**
- Baseline vs after comparison (even if expressed as a range)
- A short “Why Redwood” callout that maps to messaging pillars
- One screenshot from Console/Optimize (only if customer-approved; default is Redwood-only UI without customer data)

## Standard structure (template)
Copy/paste the following and fill in each section. If a section doesn’t apply, keep it short rather than deleting it.

### 0) Header block (top of page)
**Title (formula):** {Company} improves {primary outcome} for {workload} with Redwood {Hosted/Dedicated/Private}

**Subhead (1 sentence):** What the customer does + what changed.

**Quick facts (bullets):**
- Deployment: Hosted API / Dedicated / Private (VPC / on-prem)
- Use case(s): (e.g., chat assistant, RAG summarization, reranking, embeddings)
- Region(s): (if publishable)
- Key priorities: latency / cost / reliability / compliance / flexibility

**Results highlight (3 bullets max):**
- {Metric or range} (p95 latency, $/1M tokens, throughput, uptime)
- {Metric or range}
- {Qualitative outcome}

**Quote callout (optional):** 1–2 sentences max.

---

### 1) Customer overview (2–4 sentences)
Explain what the customer builds and why inference performance matters for their business.

Include (as allowed):
- Scale signals (requests/day, peak traffic patterns) as ranges if needed
- Why they needed to change (growth, new product line, enterprise requirements)

Avoid:
- Overly promotional language
- Specific revenue/financial figures unless explicitly approved

---

### 2) The problem (what broke / what was limiting them)
Describe the pre-Redwood state.

Prompt questions:
- What was the workload? (chat, RAG, summarization, classification, search/rerank)
- What constraints were non-negotiable? (data isolation, VPC, residency, SLA)
- What was the pain? (p95 latency, cost volatility, reliability incidents, operational overhead)

Suggested bullets:
- Latency target: {target}
- Cost target: {target}
- Reliability target: {SLO/SLA target}
- Deployment constraints: {VPC/on-prem, KMS, audit logs, etc.}

---

### 3) Requirements & constraints (what they had to solve)
List 4–8 concrete requirements.

Examples:
- Consistent p95 under {X} ms for {context length range}
- Predictable throughput during peak hours (no cold-start spikes)
- Dedicated capacity and data isolation
- Private/VPC networking + customer-managed keys (KMS/HSM)
- Audit logs + retention controls
- Safe rollouts with fallbacks

Write these as engineering constraints, not marketing claims.

---

### 4) Why Redwood (decision drivers)
Map to **approved messaging pillars** (see related page). Pick 2–3 pillars only.

Choose from:
- Performance economics (latency, throughput, $/token)
- Reliability & graceful degradation (fallbacks, multi-region, policy routing)
- Deployment flexibility (Hosted/Dedicated/Private)
- Operational tooling (Console/Optimize, rollouts, cost visibility)

Format:
- Pillar: 1 sentence claim
- Proof: 1–2 sentences describing what was validated (tests, benchmarks, pilot results)

Avoid:
- “Best-in-class” / “industry-leading” without explicit proof
- Naming competitors unless Legal and PMM approve

---

### 5) Implementation (what they built with Redwood)
This is the credibility section. Write for a senior engineer.

Include:
- Integration approach (SDK/REST, streaming, structured output, tool calling if used)
- Deployment mode details:
  - Hosted: model selection + routing policies
  - Dedicated: sizing approach (high level), autoscaling policy (if applicable), isolation notes
  - Private: networking model, key management, audit logs
- Runtime/ops features used (batching, caching, KV cache behavior, fallback policies)
- How they validated output quality (evals, prompt sets, regression monitoring)

Recommended sub-structure:
1) Architecture overview (1 paragraph)
2) Request routing + fallback (bullets)
3) Performance/cost controls (bullets)
4) Reliability + operations (bullets)

---

### 6) Architecture diagram (required)
**Goal:** Make the deployment model and request flow understandable in 30 seconds.

Diagram requirements:
- Must show: client/app → Redwood API gateway → (Hosted/Dedicated/Private runtime) → observability/Console
- If applicable, show: VPC boundary, KMS/HSM, audit log sink, caching layer, fallback route
- Use generic labels unless customer approves specifics (e.g., “Vector DB” instead of vendor name)
- No customer secrets: no internal hostnames, IPs, account IDs, ticket numbers

Ownership:
- Drafted by Solutions Engineering (or Marketing with SE review)
- Final sign-off by Solutions Engineering + customer

File handling:
- Store source (Figma/diagrams.net) and export (SVG/PNG)
- Name: {customer}-architecture-diagram-v{n}.{ext}

---

### 7) Results (proof, measurement, caveats)
Results must be both **useful** and **defensible**.

Preferred metrics (choose what matches the story):
- Latency: p50/p95 end-to-end, time-to-first-token (TTFT), tokens/sec
- Cost: $/1M tokens, infrastructure cost deltas, caching hit rate improvements
- Reliability: uptime, error rate, timeouts, incident reduction
- Throughput: requests/sec, max concurrent streams, batch sizes
- Operational impact: reduced on-call load, faster rollouts, fewer manual interventions

How to present:
- Use baseline vs after when possible.
- If exact numbers cannot be shared, publish **ranges** (e.g., “reduced p95 latency by ~30–45%”).
- Always include a short **how measured** note (time window, environment, assumptions).

**Required: Metrics disclaimer language (choose one)**
Option 1 (exact metrics allowed):
“Results are based on {customer}’s measurements in production over {time window}. Actual performance and cost will vary based on model choice, prompt structure, context length, traffic patterns, and deployment configuration.”

Option 2 (ranges/limited disclosure):
“Results are based on {customer}’s internal measurements. Metrics are presented as ranges and/or relative improvements due to confidentiality constraints. Actual performance and cost will vary based on model choice, prompt structure, context length, traffic patterns, and deployment configuration.”

Option 3 (qualitative only):
“This story describes {customer}’s implementation and qualitative outcomes. Specific metrics are not disclosed due to confidentiality constraints. Actual performance and cost will vary based on model choice, prompt structure, context length, traffic patterns, and deployment configuration.”

Do not publish:
- Vendor-provided confidential benchmarks from third parties
- Customer security findings, pen-test results, or internal audit conclusions
- Exact capacity sizing numbers if the customer requests they remain confidential

---

### 8) What’s next (CTA)
End with 1–2 concrete next steps.

Examples:
- “See how Redwood Dedicated can deliver predictable throughput for your workload.”
- “Talk to us about Private deployments for regulated environments.”
- “Run a latency/cost proof-of-value with Redwood Optimize recommendations.”

Include a link target for Sales use (contact form / dedicated landing page).

## Writing guidelines (developer-first tone)
**Do**
- Use precise language: p95, throughput, error rate, SLO, rollout, fallback
- Explain tradeoffs and constraints
- Keep claims tied to proof (“validated via…”, “measured over…”, “in a pilot with…”)
- Use short paragraphs and bullets for skimmability

**Don’t**
- Over-index on generic benefits (“transformative”, “revolutionary”)
- Overshare customer internals
- Use absolutes (“eliminated latency”, “zero downtime”) unless contractually true and approved

Voice and style
- Prefer active voice, concrete nouns, and specifics.
- Avoid Redwood jargon unless explained (e.g., briefly define “Dedicated capacity”).
- Assume technical fluency; avoid basic AI explanations.

Length guidance
- Web story: 800–1,400 words
- PDF 1-pager: 350–600 words
- Quotes: 1–2 sentences each

---

## Quote guidelines (collection, editing, approval)
Collection
- Capture quotes during interview; ask for permission to lightly edit for clarity.
- Prompt for specifics: what changed, why it matters, what they’d tell a peer.

Editing rules
- You may edit for grammar/clarity, but do not change meaning.
- Avoid inserting new claims into a quote.

Attribution
- Default: Name, Title, Company
- If restricted: First name + role, or “Engineering Lead, {Industry} company”

Approval
- Quotes require explicit customer sign-off (email confirmation is acceptable if Legal approves the consent path).

---

## Proof requirements and internal substantiation (must be logged)
For every publishable claim, ensure we have an internal reference to substantiate it.

Minimum proof sources (any 1–2 per major claim):
- Redwood Console screenshots (sanitized)
- Customer-provided metrics worksheet (approved)
- Load test results / pilot report
- Support ticket or incident postmortem summary (if relevant and publishable)
- Evals: prompt set results showing no quality regression

Internal logging
- Attach proof links in the case study working doc and reference program tracker.
- Note which items are publishable vs internal-only.

## Review + approval workflow (public case studies)
1) Draft complete (Marketing)
2) Technical validation (Solutions Engineering)
   - architecture accuracy
   - correct feature names (Console/Optimize/Dedicated/Private)
   - no unsupported technical claims
3) Messaging & claims check (Product Marketing)
4) Account alignment (AE + CSM)
   - relationship health
   - no surprises; confirm readiness for reference usage
5) Legal review (logo/quotes/attribution/metrics disclosure)
6) Customer final review
   - target: 5–10 business days unless otherwise agreed
7) Publish (Docs/Web) + enablement updates (Sales/CS)

SLA guidance
- Plan for 2–3 review cycles; do not commit to a publish date until Legal and customer review windows are confirmed.

---

## Common pitfalls (and how to avoid them)
- Pitfall: “Results” section is vague.
  - Fix: add ranges + how-measured note + disclaimer.

- Pitfall: Diagram shows too much detail.
  - Fix: use generic system names and remove network specifics.

- Pitfall: Claims drift beyond what was measured.
  - Fix: anchor each claim to a proof source and keep wording conservative.

- Pitfall: Customer feels overburdened.
  - Fix: consolidate questions, share a preview outline, and keep approval cycles predictable.
