Case Study Messaging Pillars (Customer Stories)

h1. Purpose
This page defines the standard messaging framework for Redwood Inference customer stories (public case studies, 1-pagers, sales talk tracks, and reference-call briefs). It provides:

* The four core story pillars to use consistently across customer narratives.
* Approved claim language (what we can say, and how to say it).
* Proof requirements for each claim type (what evidence is needed for *public* vs *sales-only* use).
* Guardrails for regulated customers and confidential metrics.

This framework is designed to keep our stories:

* Developer-first and technically credible.
* Consistent across Hosted API, Dedicated, and Private deployments.
* Safe for Legal/Brand approval and customer review.

h1. Audience
* Product Marketing and Marketing writers
* AEs/SEs using stories in late-stage deals
* CSMs coordinating references
* Solutions Engineering validating technical claims

h1. How to use this framework
1. Pick 1–2 primary pillars and 1 supporting pillar for the story.
2. Select approved claim language *as written* (or minor edits that do not change meaning).
3. Attach proof artifacts early (before customer interview #2 and before Legal outreach).
4. If a claim needs proof you do not have, either (a) downgrade it to a softer claim, (b) move it to “sales-only,” or (c) remove it.

h1. Pillars overview (one-slide summary)
*Pillar 1: Performance economics* — Faster and/or cheaper inference at production quality (latency, throughput, $/token, utilization).

*Pillar 2: Reliability + graceful degradation* — Stay online under load and during incidents via routing policies, fallbacks, and operational controls.

*Pillar 3: Deployment flexibility* — Same interface across Hosted, Dedicated, and Private/VPC/on-prem constraints.

*Pillar 4: Operational tooling* — Console and Optimize reduce operational burden: observability, rollouts, cost controls, and quality regression monitoring.

h1. Pillar 1 — Performance economics
h2. What we mean
Customers choose Redwood when they need predictable performance and cost at scale, not just “model access.” Stories should tie improvements to specific production constraints: p95 latency targets, throughput ceilings, GPU utilization, unit economics, or headcount/time-to-ship.

h2. Approved claims (safe defaults)
Use these when you have directional evidence but not publishable exact metrics.

* “Improved p95 latency and stabilized performance under real production traffic.”
* “Reduced cost per token / improved unit economics through batching, caching, and right-sized deployments.”
* “Reached predictable throughput targets without rebuilding the serving stack.”
* “Scaled usage while keeping latency within SLOs.”

h2. Strong claims (require specific proof)
Only use if the proof requirements below are met.

* “Reduced p95 latency by X% / from A ms to B ms.”
* “Reduced $/1M tokens by X% / from $A to $B.”
* “Increased throughput by X% / from A req/s to B req/s at target context lengths.”
* “Achieved Y% GPU utilization improvement via continuous batching / KV caching.”

h2. Proof requirements (Performance economics)
*Public (case study page / PDF)*
* Customer-approved metrics with measurement method and timeframe.
* Baseline comparison source (before Redwood): prior provider dashboard export, internal benchmark, or agreed proxy baseline.
* Redwood-side evidence: Console screenshot (redacted) or SE-validated metric export.
* If exact numbers are sensitive: customer-approved ranges (e.g., “20–30%”) plus a short methodology note.

*Sales-only (deck / verbal / reference call prep)*
* CSM or AE confirmation + internal dashboards acceptable.
* Include caveats: workload shape, model version, and measurement window.

h2. Required caveats (include when relevant)
* “Results vary by model, prompt/context length, and traffic shape.”
* “Latency reported as p95 unless otherwise stated.”
* “Cost comparisons include infrastructure + serving overhead where applicable; customer methodology documented.”

h1. Pillar 2 — Reliability + graceful degradation
h2. What we mean
Reliability is not only uptime; it is maintaining acceptable behavior when traffic spikes, capacity changes, or dependencies fail. Customer stories should describe the operational posture: SLOs, fallbacks, routing policies, and incident learnings.

h2. Approved claims (safe defaults)
* “Improved reliability through routing policies and operational controls.”
* “Reduced impact of capacity constraints by using fallback model variants / regions (where applicable).”
* “Maintained application availability during traffic spikes via autoscaling and throttling policies.”
* “De-risked production rollouts with canaries and automatic fallback policies.”

h2. Strong claims (require specific proof)
* “Achieved X% uptime over Y months.”
* “Reduced error rate by X%.”
* “Reduced incident impact (MTTR) by X% / from A to B.”

h2. Proof requirements (Reliability)
*Public*
* Customer-approved reliability statements.
* If quoting uptime/availability: include timeframe and what is measured (API availability vs end-to-end app availability).
* If referencing fallbacks/routing: SE review of the routing policy description (no over-specific implementation claims that vary by deployment).
* If referencing compliance-grade reliability: confirm with Security/Legal that wording does not imply contractual SLA unless the customer contract includes it.

*Sales-only*
* Internal incident postmortem link or SRE ticket references are allowed, but do not share customer-identifying details outside Redwood.

h2. Language to avoid
* Avoid absolute guarantees unless contractually true: “zero downtime,” “never fails,” “always.”
* Avoid implying a universal SLA: “Redwood guarantees 99.99% uptime” unless the specific package and contract support it.

h1. Pillar 3 — Deployment flexibility (Hosted / Dedicated / Private)
h2. What we mean
Customers want a consistent runtime and developer experience across environments. The story should connect deployment choice to a real constraint: data isolation, regionality, security controls, predictable throughput, or procurement/compliance.

h2. Approved claims (safe defaults)
* “Deployed on Redwood Dedicated to secure predictable throughput and data isolation.”
* “Chose Redwood Private (VPC) to meet security and compliance requirements without sacrificing developer experience.”
* “Kept the same API surface while changing deployment mode as requirements evolved.”
* “Used Redwood-hosted API for fastest time-to-value, then expanded into Dedicated/Private for scale or compliance.”

h2. Strong claims (require specific proof)
* “Met specific regulatory requirements (e.g., HIPAA, SOC 2, data residency) due to Redwood Private.”
* “Enabled customer-managed key management (KMS/HSM) for encryption controls.”
* “Supported air-gapped/on-prem deployment.”

h2. Proof requirements (Deployment flexibility)
*Public*
* Legal-approved wording for compliance-related claims.
* If mentioning a regulation/framework: confirm the customer is comfortable being associated with the requirement and that our statement is accurate (do not imply certification we do not have).
* If mentioning key management/audit logs: confirm exact product behavior with Security/SE (format, retention, access controls).

*Sales-only*
* Architecture diagram (redacted) reviewed by SE.

h2. Language to avoid
* “Certified for [framework]” unless explicitly true and current.
* “HIPAA compliant” as a blanket statement—use: “supports HIPAA-aligned deployments” or customer-approved equivalent, and only when validated.

h1. Pillar 4 — Operational tooling (Console + Optimize)
h2. What we mean
Operational tooling is where teams feel the day-2 value: observability, cost controls, rollouts, and quality regression prevention. Stories should show how teams operate Redwood, not just how they integrated it.

h2. Approved claims (safe defaults)
* “Used the Console to understand cost and latency by route/model and troubleshoot errors faster.”
* “Used rollout controls (canary/A-B/fallback) to deploy model changes with reduced risk.”
* “Used Optimize recommendations to tune batching/caching and reduce unit cost.”
* “Improved operational confidence with dashboards, tracing hooks, and usage analytics.”

h2. Strong claims (require specific proof)
* “Reduced engineering time spent on inference operations by X%.”
* “Caught quality regressions before they reached production using evals/prompt sets.”

h2. Proof requirements (Operational tooling)
*Public*
* Customer quote or customer-approved description of their workflow.
* If referencing specific UI features: confirm naming and behavior matches current product.
* Any screenshots must be redacted and approved by the customer (and reviewed by Legal if they reveal sensitive context).

*Sales-only*
* Internal screenshots OK if the customer name/account details are removed.

h1. Proof system: what counts as “enough”
Use the table below to determine what is required before publishing.

|| Proof type || Examples || Allowed for public? || Notes ||
| Customer quote approval | Email confirmation or signed consent, with exact quote text | Yes | Required for attributed quotes. |
| Customer metric approval | Written approval of metric values or ranges | Yes | If not approved, do not publish numbers. |
| Redwood Console export/screenshot | p95 latency view, cost by route/model | Yes (redacted) | Must remove identifiers and prompts; customer must approve. |
| SE validation | Architecture diagram review, routing/caching behavior | Yes | Use for technical correctness, not as a substitute for customer consent. |
| Internal dashboards only | SRE metrics, billing reports | No (public) | Sales-only at most; do not include in published assets. |

h1. Metrics guidance (how to present results credibly)
h2. Preferred metrics
* Latency: p50 and p95 (p99 if relevant), include context length and streaming vs non-streaming.
* Cost: $/1M tokens (or customer’s preferred unit), include what is included/excluded.
* Reliability: availability definition + window, error rate, or incident impact.
* Throughput: requests/sec at given token rate; or tokens/sec for consistent workloads.

h2. When customers cannot share exact numbers
Use one of these patterns:
* Ranges: “20–30% reduction in p95 latency.”
* Relative statement: “double-digit latency improvement.” (Use sparingly; still requires evidence.)
* Outcome-based: “met internal latency SLOs for the first time at peak traffic.”

h2. Required methodology note (public)
Include a short “How measured” footnote when any numeric claim appears:
* timeframe (e.g., “over 30 days of production traffic”)
* scope (which endpoints/routes)
* baseline source (prior provider or pre-optimization state)

h1. Voice and narrative guidelines (developer-first)
* Lead with the problem constraints (latency SLO, security boundary, scaling pain).
* Be specific about the implementation (routing policy, batching, caching, rollout approach) without exposing sensitive internals.
* Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over buzzwords: “continuous batching,” “KV cache,” “canary rollout,” “audit logs,” “KMS integration.”
* Avoid vendor dunking; focus on why Redwood worked for their requirements.

h1. Legal/brand guardrails (non-negotiables)
* Any logo usage, customer name, and attributed quotes require executed consent (or documented equivalent per Legal).
* Do not publish:
  * customer prompts, proprietary data, or model outputs that can reveal sensitive information
  * contract terms (pricing, SLA details) unless the customer explicitly approves and Legal signs off
  * claims that imply certification, endorsement, or partnership without written approval
* If the customer requests anonymization, align early on the acceptable naming format (e.g., “Top-5 U.S. insurer”) and ensure consistency across web/PDF/deck.

h1. Appendix A — Copy blocks (approved, reusable)
h2. Short “Why Redwood” paragraph (general)
“Redwood Inference helps teams run LLM inference with predictable latency, cost, and reliability across hosted, dedicated, and private deployments. Customers use Redwood’s high-throughput runtime plus Console and Optimize tooling to tune unit economics, operate deployments confidently, and ship changes safely with rollouts and fallback policies.”

h2. Dedicated-oriented positioning (1–2 sentences)
“Redwood Dedicated provides predictable throughput and data isolation while keeping the same developer experience as the hosted API. Teams use Dedicated when they need consistent performance at scale and clear operational controls without building and operating a serving stack internally.”

h2. Private/VPC-oriented positioning (1–2 sentences)
“Redwood Private brings Redwood’s inference platform into a customer’s controlled network boundary (VPC and optional on-prem) for regulated or security-sensitive workloads. Teams keep the same serving interface and operational tooling while meeting stricter networking, access control, and audit requirements.”

h1. Appendix B — Review checklist (before sending to customer)
* Story uses 1–2 primary pillars and does not over-claim.
* All attributed quotes are final and approved.
* Any numbers are either (a) customer-approved exact values, or (b) customer-approved ranges + methodology note.
* Technical sections reviewed by Solutions Engineering.
* Compliance/security language reviewed by Legal (and Security when needed).
* Screenshots/diagrams redacted and safe.
