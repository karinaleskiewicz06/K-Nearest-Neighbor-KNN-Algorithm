Status Page Template — Regional Impairment, Regional Failover, and Planned Failover Tests

## Purpose
This page provides copy-and-paste templates for Redwood Inference status page updates during:
- Unplanned regional impairment (partial or full).
- Unplanned automated/manual regional failover.
- Planned controlled failover tests (“game days”).

It also standardizes update cadence and recommended wording so we communicate clearly without over-promising or exposing sensitive internal details.

## Audience and tone
- Audience: customers and end users.
- Tone: factual, calm, action-oriented.
- Avoid: internal system names, speculative root cause, blame, or unverified ETAs.

## Ownership and workflow (quick)
**Primary drafter:** Incident Commander (IC) or Comms Lead (often Support liaison).

**Approver (external-facing):**
- Severity 1/2 unplanned: IC approval required.
- Planned tests: SRE Program Lead or designated on-call approver.

**Where to post:**
- Status Page (customer-facing).
- Optional: Console banner/status widget is separate; coordinate with Product/Console owner.

## What to include in every update
- What customers may observe (errors, timeouts, increased latency).
- Which products are impacted (Hosted API, Dedicated, Console, etc.).
- A clear action being taken (investigating, mitigating, failing over, monitoring).
- Next update time (even if no change).

## What NOT to include
- “All systems down” unless verified.
- Unconfirmed root cause (“cloud provider outage”) unless corroborated.
- Internal region codes, routing policy names, incident channels, ticket numbers.
- Specific customer names (unless pre-approved and written as general guidance).

## Component selection guidance
Select the most accurate component(s) in the status page tool:
- **Hosted API** (inference endpoints, streaming)
- **Dedicated** (tenant-isolated capacity pools)
- **Console** (UI, dashboards)
- **Auth / Control Plane** (only if customer-facing auth/control plane issues are confirmed)

If impact is limited to a subset of models/routes, still mark the top-level product component and clarify in text.

## Update cadence
Use this as the default cadence unless a comms lead overrides for a specific event.

**Unplanned impairment (suspected or confirmed):**
- Initial post: within 10 minutes of confirmation of customer impact.
- During active mitigation/failover: every 15 minutes.
- Stable but not fully resolved: every 30–60 minutes.
- Resolved: within 15 minutes of resolution confirmation.

**Planned failover test:**
- Start notice: 24–72 hours prior (enterprise accounts may get direct notice separately).
- “Test starting” update: at start window.
- In-window update: every 30 minutes (or immediately if unexpected customer impact occurs).
- Completion update: at end window.

## Template variables
Replace placeholders consistently:
- {REGION}: impacted region (e.g., US East, EU West)
- {PRODUCTS}: Hosted API / Dedicated / Console
- {IMPACT}: what customers see (e.g., elevated 5xx, timeouts, increased latency)
- {START_TIME_TZ}: start timestamp with timezone (e.g., 14:05 UTC)
- {NEXT_UPDATE_TIME_TZ}: next scheduled update
- {WORKAROUND}: optional; only if validated (e.g., retry with exponential backoff)
- {SCOPE}: optional scope qualifiers (subset of traffic, certain endpoints)

---

# A) Unplanned Regional Impairment (Investigating)
**Status:** Investigating

**Title:** Regional Service Disruption in {REGION}

**Body (recommended):**
We are investigating an issue affecting {PRODUCTS} in {REGION}. Customers may experience {IMPACT} starting around {START_TIME_TZ}. Our engineering team is actively working to identify the cause and mitigate impact.

Next update by {NEXT_UPDATE_TIME_TZ}.

**Optional additions (use only if true):**
- Impact scope qualifier: “This appears to affect a subset of requests.”
- Workaround: “If you are able, retry requests with exponential backoff.”

**Do not include (yet):** mention of failover unless it is actively occurring or confirmed as the mitigation path.

---

# B) Unplanned Regional Impairment (Identified / Mitigating)
**Status:** Identified or Monitoring (choose based on confidence)

**Title:** Mitigation in Progress for {PRODUCTS} in {REGION}

**Body (recommended):**
We have identified a service issue impacting {PRODUCTS} in {REGION}. Customers may experience {IMPACT}. We are applying mitigations to restore normal service and are monitoring recovery.

Next update by {NEXT_UPDATE_TIME_TZ}.

**Optional additions:**
- If a specific symptom is verified: “We are seeing elevated error rates and increased request latency.”
- If relevant to Dedicated: “Dedicated capacity customers in {REGION} may see reduced throughput during mitigation.”

---

# C) Unplanned Regional Failover (Failover in progress)
Use this when we are actively shifting traffic to another region (automated or manually approved).

**Status:** Monitoring

**Title:** Regional Failover in Progress for {PRODUCTS} from {REGION}

**Body (recommended):**
We are performing a regional failover for {PRODUCTS} due to an impairment in {REGION}. During this process, some customers may experience {IMPACT} or brief reconnects as traffic is routed to alternate capacity.

We are monitoring service health and capacity in the destination region(s) and will provide updates as the failover completes.

Next update by {NEXT_UPDATE_TIME_TZ}.

**Optional additions (use only if accurate):**
- Streaming note: “Streaming sessions may disconnect and require client reconnection.”
- Retry guidance: “Retry with exponential backoff; avoid aggressive immediate retries.”

**Avoid:** naming the destination region(s) unless there is a clear customer requirement (e.g., data residency communications) and Security has approved the wording.

---

# D) Dedicated-specific note (use as an add-on line)
Add this line when Dedicated pools are involved and customers might notice drain/evacuation behavior:

**Add-on sentence:**
Dedicated customers may observe temporary reductions in throughput or increased queueing while capacity is rebalanced.

Do not describe internal drain tooling; keep it customer-observable.

---

# E) Resolution (Recovered)
**Status:** Resolved

**Title:** Resolved — {PRODUCTS} in {REGION}

**Body (recommended):**
This issue has been resolved and {PRODUCTS} in {REGION} have returned to normal operation. Customers should no longer experience {IMPACT}.

If you continue to see issues, please contact Support and include request IDs and approximate timestamps.

**Optional additions:**
- If failover occurred: “Traffic has been stabilized and we are continuing to monitor performance.”

---

# F) Planned Controlled Failover Test (Advance Notice)
Use this for scheduled game days / controlled tests with limited blast radius.

**Status:** Scheduled

**Title:** Planned Regional Failover Test — {PRODUCTS} ({DATE_RANGE_TZ})

**Body (recommended):**
We will be performing a planned controlled failover test for {PRODUCTS} during the window {DATE_RANGE_TZ}. The goal of this test is to validate regional resiliency and reduce customer impact during real regional events.

During the test window, a subset of requests may experience brief {IMPACT} (for example, short-lived increases in latency or transient errors). We do not expect sustained disruption.

We will post updates here during the test and confirm completion.

**Notes:**
- Keep expected impact conservative.
- If you genuinely expect “no impact,” still avoid absolute guarantees; use “we do not expect customer impact.”

---

# G) Planned Controlled Failover Test (Starting)
**Status:** In Progress (or Monitoring depending on tooling)

**Title:** In Progress — Planned Regional Failover Test for {PRODUCTS}

**Body (recommended):**
Our planned controlled failover test for {PRODUCTS} is now in progress. We are monitoring service health closely.

Next update by {NEXT_UPDATE_TIME_TZ}.

---

# H) Planned Controlled Failover Test (Unexpected impact escalation)
Use this if impact is worse than expected. This message intentionally shifts from “test” framing to customer impact framing.

**Status:** Investigating or Identified

**Title:** Investigating — Elevated Errors/Latency During Planned Failover Test

**Body (recommended):**
During our planned controlled failover test, we are observing higher-than-expected impact to {PRODUCTS}. Customers may experience {IMPACT}.

We are pausing or rolling back the test as needed to reduce impact and will provide an update by {NEXT_UPDATE_TIME_TZ}.

---

# I) Planned Controlled Failover Test (Completed)
**Status:** Completed

**Title:** Completed — Planned Regional Failover Test for {PRODUCTS}

**Body (recommended):**
Our planned controlled failover test for {PRODUCTS} has completed. We are continuing to monitor service health.

Thank you for your patience while we validate improvements to regional resiliency.

---

## Standard “Next update” times
Pick a specific time; do not say “soon.” Examples:
- “Next update by 15:30 UTC.”
- “Next update within 30 minutes (by 15:30 UTC).”

## Quick checklist before posting
- Confirm impact is customer-visible (dashboard + on-call confirmation).
- Confirm correct components selected.
- Ensure placeholders replaced.
- Ensure no internal names or speculation.
- Ensure next update time is included.

## Examples (filled)
**Example — Unplanned impairment:**
We are investigating an issue affecting Hosted API in US East. Customers may experience elevated error rates and timeouts starting around 14:05 UTC. Our engineering team is actively working to identify the cause and mitigate impact.

Next update by 14:30 UTC.

**Example — Failover in progress:**
We are performing a regional failover for Hosted API due to an impairment in US East. During this process, some customers may experience brief increases in latency, transient errors, or reconnects as traffic is routed to alternate capacity.

Next update by 15:00 UTC.
